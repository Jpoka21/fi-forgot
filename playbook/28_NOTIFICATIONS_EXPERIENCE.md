# Notifications Experience

## Purpose

Notifications are one of the most powerful, and most dangerous, parts of a Relationship Concierge.

A notification is an interruption.

Every interruption asks the user to stop what they are doing and give the concierge their attention.

Because attention is finite, every notification spends trust.

Most software treats notifications as engagement tools.

F.I. Forgot does not.

A world class Relationship Concierge communicates only when communication genuinely improves the user's ability to care for someone they love.

Its objective is never to increase opens, clicks, sessions, or retention metrics.

Its objective is to help someone become more thoughtful.

Sometimes that means notifying immediately.

Sometimes it means waiting.

Sometimes it means combining multiple messages.

Sometimes it means saying nothing at all.

Silence is often the highest quality communication.

This document defines how the Relationship Concierge communicates once it has already decided that communication is appropriate.

Document 26 defines *whether* the concierge should proactively help.

This document defines *how* that help reaches the user.

---

# Notification Philosophy

Every notification should feel like it came from someone who knows the user personally.

It should never feel like software.

It should never feel like marketing.

It should never feel like automation.

Instead, every communication should feel thoughtful.

Helpful.

Timely.

Respectful.

Calm.

A great concierge does not constantly seek attention.

A great concierge quietly appears exactly when needed.

The user should gradually develop a feeling that the concierge always seems to know the right moment to reach out.

That feeling creates trust.

Trust creates long term engagement.

Notifications should therefore optimize for trust rather than frequency.

---

# Communication Principles

Every notification should satisfy five principles.

## 1. It must have a purpose.

Every communication should help the user accomplish something meaningful.

If nothing meaningful changes, nothing should be sent.

---

## 2. It must respect attention.

The user's attention is valuable.

Interruptions should be rare.

Notifications should never compete with one another.

The concierge should avoid sending multiple messages that could have been combined.

---

## 3. It must reduce effort.

Communication should make relationships easier.

It should never create unnecessary work.

If a notification generates anxiety instead of clarity, it has failed.

---

## 4. It must increase confidence.

After reading a notification, the user should feel more prepared.

Not more overwhelmed.

---

## 5. It must disappear when unnecessary.

Once a notification has served its purpose, it should quietly fade into history.

The concierge should never continually remind users about completed tasks.

Relationships move forward.

The interface should too.

---

# Notifications Are Conversations

Traditional applications send alerts.

Relationship Concierges continue conversations.

Every notification exists within an ongoing relationship between the concierge and the user.

Because of this, messages should feel connected over time.

Instead of repeating information, communications should acknowledge previous interactions.

For example:

"I found another birthday coming up."

is weaker than:

"You already sent Emma's birthday card. Your next important moment is your dad's anniversary."

The second demonstrates awareness.

Awareness creates confidence.

Confidence creates trust.

---

# The Hierarchy of Communication

Not every event deserves the same level of interruption.

Communication should scale according to importance.

The hierarchy should always move from the least intrusive option capable of accomplishing the goal.

## Level 1

Silent updates.

Examples include:

Relationship health recalculations.

Timeline updates.

AI memory organization.

Background learning.

Profile improvements.

Draft optimization.

No notification is required.

The concierge simply becomes smarter.

---

## Level 2

Passive interface updates.

Examples include:

New suggestions waiting.

Improved relationship insights.

Additional memory recommendations.

Recently completed background work.

These appear naturally when the user opens the application.

No interruption occurs.

---

## Level 3

In app messages.

These are visible while actively using the application.

Examples include:

Helpful suggestions.

Relationship coaching.

Memory prompts.

Card improvements.

New concierge recommendations.

Because the user already has the application open, no external communication is necessary.

---

## Level 4

Digest communication.

Multiple helpful updates are combined into one communication.

Rather than sending four separate notifications throughout the day, the concierge sends one thoughtful summary.

This significantly reduces interruption while increasing usefulness.

---

## Level 5

Standard notifications.

These are individual communications that deserve immediate attention.

Examples include:

Upcoming birthdays.

Anniversary preparation.

Cards needing review.

Shipping deadlines.

Delivery issues.

Important recipient updates.

These should be uncommon.

---

## Level 6

High priority notifications.

These represent situations where delaying communication creates real consequences.

Examples include:

A card can no longer arrive on time.

Address verification is urgently needed.

Payment failed before mailing.

Recipient delivery failed.

Critical action required.

These should be extremely rare.

The concierge earns the right to interrupt by doing so only when truly necessary.

---

# Notification Confidence

The concierge should understand how confident it is before interrupting the user.

Low confidence recommendations should remain inside the application.

Medium confidence suggestions may appear in digests.

High confidence opportunities may justify notifications.

Near certainty may justify immediate communication.

Confidence should be influenced by factors including:

Relationship importance.

Upcoming event significance.

Shipping deadlines.

Recipient history.

User responsiveness.

Past behavior.

Current context.

Urgency.

Potential consequences.

The more uncertain the concierge becomes, the quieter it should become.

---

# Respecting Attention

Attention is a finite resource.

Every unnecessary interruption slightly weakens the relationship between the concierge and the user.

The concierge should therefore assume the user is busy.

Busy people appreciate brevity.

Every notification should answer three questions immediately.

What happened?

Why does it matter?

What should I do?

Nothing else belongs in a notification.

Long explanations belong inside the application.

Notifications simply create awareness.

# Communication Channels

Every communication channel has strengths.

Choosing the correct channel is as important as deciding to communicate at all.

## Push Notifications

Push notifications are best for:

Upcoming deadlines.

Time sensitive reminders.

Shipping milestones.

Urgent decisions.

Cards requiring approval.

Immediate attention.

Push notifications should be concise.

They should communicate one idea.

They should encourage action without creating stress.

---

## Email

Email is appropriate for information the user may want to revisit.

Examples include:

Weekly relationship summaries.

Monthly concierge reports.

Relationship insights.

Completed accomplishments.

Upcoming planning.

Seasonal preparation.

Emails can provide richer context.

They are not interruptions.

They are references.

The concierge should use email when information benefits from permanence.

---

## SMS

SMS is the most intrusive communication channel available.

It should be used sparingly.

Examples include:

Critical delivery failures.

Same day shipping deadlines.

Urgent payment issues.

Time sensitive address verification.

Events where waiting until tomorrow creates a negative outcome.

Marketing should never be sent through SMS.

Routine reminders should never use SMS.

Relationship coaching should never use SMS.

SMS exists only for moments where immediacy genuinely matters.

---

## In App Messaging

In app communication should be the concierge's preferred teaching environment.

While the user is already engaged, the concierge can provide:

Suggestions.

Coaching.

Relationship insights.

Memory recommendations.

Conversation starters.

Celebration moments.

Educational guidance.

This avoids unnecessary interruptions while still providing tremendous value.

---

## Silent Learning

Many concierge activities deserve no communication whatsoever.

Examples include:

Learning preferences.

Improving AI understanding.

Organizing memories.

Updating relationship scores.

Optimizing future cards.

Categorizing interests.

Improving personalization.

The concierge simply becomes better over time.

The user experiences the improvement naturally.

No announcement is necessary.

# Choosing the Right Channel

The concierge should not ask, "Can we notify the user?"

It should ask, "What is the least intrusive channel that still helps the user succeed?"

The correct channel depends on urgency, importance, complexity, and whether action is required.

## Use silent updates when:

The user does not need to know immediately.

The improvement will be visible later.

No action is required.

The information is not emotionally important.

Examples:

A relationship score changed slightly.

A memory was categorized.

A card draft improved in the background.

A recipient profile became more complete.

---

## Use in app messaging when:

The user is already present.

The message is helpful but not urgent.

The topic benefits from context.

The user may want to explore or refine something.

Examples:

"Your note about Emma's new job could make her birthday card feel more personal."

"You mentioned your dad loves old Mets stories. Want to save that as a memory?"

"Your relationship with Mike has a few strong memories, but no recent updates."

---

## Use email when:

The message is useful but not urgent.

The user may want to reference it later.

The information contains multiple items.

The communication is more reflective than immediate.

Examples:

Weekly relationship digest.

Monthly concierge summary.

Upcoming events for the next 30 days.

Seasonal relationship planning.

Relationship wins.

---

## Use push notifications when:

The timing matters.

The action is simple.

The consequence of waiting is meaningful.

The message can be understood quickly.

Examples:

"Your mom's birthday card needs approval today to arrive on time."

"Alex's thank you card is ready to review."

"Your anniversary card ships tomorrow."

---

## Use SMS when:

The matter is urgent.

The consequence is real.

Other channels are likely insufficient.

The user has explicitly permitted SMS.

Examples:

"Your card cannot be mailed unless the address is confirmed today."

"Payment failed, and your anniversary card will miss the mailing window unless fixed today."

SMS should feel like an emergency tap on the shoulder.

Not a feature.

# Timing Philosophy

The right message at the wrong time is still the wrong message.

A Relationship Concierge should understand that people live busy, uneven lives.

They wake up late.

They are in meetings.

They are driving.

They are with family.

They are sleeping.

They are overwhelmed.

The concierge should communicate when the user is most likely to appreciate the help, not merely when the system detects an event.

Timing should feel human.

A good concierge does not call at dinner unless something is urgent.

A good concierge does not remind someone about a birthday at midnight.

A good concierge does not send three messages before breakfast.

F.I. Forgot should behave the same way.

---

# Default Timing Rules

The concierge should prefer calm, usable windows.

For most users, the best default communication windows are:

Morning planning.

Early afternoon review.

Early evening personal time.

The system should avoid late night interruptions unless the matter is urgent.

It should avoid very early morning interruptions unless the user has demonstrated that they are active at that time.

It should avoid sending emotional or relationship related prompts during obviously transactional or stressful windows when possible.

The goal is not simply delivery.

The goal is receptivity.

---

# Quiet Hours

Quiet hours are a core trust feature.

They should not be treated as a settings checkbox buried in preferences.

The concierge should behave as though quiet hours exist even before the user configures them.

By default, F.I. Forgot should avoid non urgent external notifications during late night and early morning hours.

Urgent notifications may still be sent when the consequence of silence is worse than the interruption.

Examples of urgent exceptions:

A card deadline expires today.

A payment failure prevents mailing.

A delivery address must be confirmed immediately.

A card has failed to send.

The concierge should never abuse urgent exceptions.

If everything is urgent, nothing is urgent.

---

# Time Zones

Relationship moments are deeply tied to time.

Birthdays, anniversaries, holidays, and delivery windows all depend on geography.

The concierge should account for the user's time zone and, where relevant, the recipient's time zone.

A birthday reminder should feel early enough to act but not awkwardly timed.

A Mother's Day prompt should account for where the user is.

A delivery deadline should be based on the mailing requirements of the destination.

Time zone awareness should happen quietly.

The user should not need to manage this manually unless something is unclear.

---

# Frequency Philosophy

The concierge should communicate less often than a typical app.

Frequency should be earned through usefulness.

A user who finds notifications helpful may welcome more.

A user who ignores notifications should receive fewer.

A user who dismisses reminders should not be chased endlessly.

The concierge should adapt over time.

Notification volume should respond to:

User engagement.

Relationship importance.

Number of recipients.

Upcoming event density.

Past dismissal behavior.

Channel preferences.

Successful outcomes.

Missed deadlines.

No user should feel like the concierge is nagging them.

The concierge should feel like a thoughtful assistant who knows when to step back.

---

# Intelligent Batching

Multiple communications should be combined whenever possible.

If three relationships need attention this week, the concierge should usually send one helpful planning message instead of three separate reminders.

Batching should be used for:

Upcoming events.

Relationship health suggestions.

Memory prompts.

Low urgency tasks.

Weekly planning.

Seasonal preparation.

However, batching should not hide urgency.

A card that needs approval today should not be buried inside a weekly digest.

The concierge should separate true deadlines from general relationship guidance.

The principle is simple:

Combine what can wait.

Separate what cannot.

---

# Escalation

Escalation should be rare, predictable, and justified.

The concierge may escalate when:

The user has not responded.

The event is important.

The deadline is approaching.

The consequence of inaction is meaningful.

The action required is clear.

Escalation should move gradually.

For example:

In app suggestion.

Email reminder.

Push notification.

High priority push.

SMS only if explicitly allowed and truly necessary.

Escalation should never feel punitive.

It should feel protective.

The tone should be:

"I do not want you to miss this."

Not:

"You failed to respond."

---

# Reminder Philosophy

Reminders should not repeat the same message.

Each reminder should add context, clarity, or urgency.

Bad reminder:

"Reminder, your card is due."

Better reminder:

"Your dad's birthday card needs approval today to arrive by Friday."

The second message explains the consequence.

The concierge should avoid sending repeated reminders that do not change.

If the user ignores a low importance reminder, the concierge should usually stop.

If the user ignores a high importance reminder, the concierge may escalate once or twice.

After that, silence may be more respectful.

The concierge should learn from non response.

Ignoring is a signal.

---

# Notification Copy

Notification copy should be warm, clear, and calm.

It should avoid hype.

It should avoid guilt.

It should avoid false urgency.

It should avoid generic app language.

Notifications should sound like a thoughtful concierge.

Not a dashboard.

Not a marketing team.

Not a robot.

## Preferred tone

Calm.

Specific.

Helpful.

Human.

Confident.

Brief.

## Avoid

"Don't forget!"

"Act now!"

"Last chance!"

"You need to..."

"We noticed you haven't..."

"Engage with your relationship health dashboard."

"Your recipient profile is incomplete."

These phrases feel transactional or scolding.

## Better

"Your anniversary card is ready to review."

"Your mom's birthday is coming up. I prepared a draft."

"One card needs approval today to arrive on time."

"You have a few thoughtful moments worth saving."

"Emma's new job would be a great detail to include."

---

# Emotional Sensitivity

Relationship communication can touch grief, distance, tension, estrangement, illness, regret, and complicated family dynamics.

The concierge should never assume every event is happy.

Birthdays may be painful.

Anniversaries may be complicated.

Mother's Day and Father's Day can be sensitive.

A recipient may have passed away.

A relationship may be strained.

A world class Relationship Concierge communicates with emotional intelligence.

This means notifications should avoid overly cheerful assumptions.

Instead of:

"Celebrate your amazing dad this Father's Day!"

Use:

"Father's Day is coming up. I can help you decide whether to send something."

This leaves space for complexity.

The concierge should never force warmth where the relationship data suggests caution.

---

# When Silence Is Better

Silence is not failure.

Silence can be the most thoughtful action.

The concierge should remain silent when:

The information is not actionable.

The user has already handled the task.

The system lacks confidence.

The message would create more anxiety than help.

The relationship context is sensitive.

The timing is poor.

The user has recently ignored similar messages.

The communication would feel self serving.

The concierge should never communicate merely to prove that it is working.

The best concierge often works quietly.

The user should feel the benefit without constantly hearing from the system.
