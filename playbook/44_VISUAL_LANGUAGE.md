# F.I. Forgot Visual Language

---

# Purpose

This document defines the visual language of F.I. Forgot.

While the Copy Guide defines how the product speaks, this document defines how the product looks and feels.

Visual design is not decoration.

Every design decision should reinforce the feeling that the user has a trusted Relationship Concierge quietly working behind the scenes.

The interface should never compete for attention.

It should create space for relationships to be the focus.

Whenever a visual decision is made, ask:

> **Does this feel like a world class Relationship Concierge?**

If the answer is no, redesign it.

---

# Design Philosophy

F.I. Forgot is designed around people, not software.

The interface should disappear into the background while relationships remain at the center.

The experience should feel:

* Calm

* Premium

* Human

* Warm

* Spacious

* Organized

* Effortless

* Trustworthy

Users should never feel overwhelmed.

Instead, they should feel quietly supported.

---

# Emotional Goals

Every screen should create one or more of these emotions.

## Calm

Nothing should feel rushed.

Generous spacing.

Minimal distractions.

Soft movement.

Clear hierarchy.

---

## Confidence

The product always feels intentional.

Nothing appears accidental.

Everything aligns.

Everything has purpose.

---

## Warmth

Relationships are emotional.

The interface should acknowledge this without becoming sentimental.

Warm colors.

Soft corners.

Friendly illustrations.

Human photography only when appropriate.

---

## Trust

Users are sharing meaningful memories.

The design must communicate safety.

Simple layouts.

Predictable navigation.

Transparent interactions.

Nothing hidden.

Nothing manipulative.

---

# Visual Personality

Imagine a concierge desk inside a luxury boutique hotel.

Not flashy.

Not sterile.

Quietly beautiful.

Organized.

Inviting.

Every element feels intentional.

The product should never resemble:

* enterprise software

* CRM platforms

* banking software

* social media

* productivity dashboards

* AI tools

It should feel unique.

---

# Visual Principles

## Simplicity

Remove unnecessary elements.

If something doesn't help the user, remove it.

---

## Breathing Room

Whitespace is a feature.

Never fill space simply because it exists.

The product should feel easy to read.

---

## Hierarchy

The user's eye should naturally know where to look first.

Primary content should always dominate.

Secondary information should quietly support it.

---

## Consistency

The same interaction should always look and behave the same way.

Users should never have to relearn the interface.

---

## Human Centered

People are the hero.

Features support people.

Not the other way around.

---

# White Space Philosophy

Whitespace communicates confidence.

Premium products rarely feel crowded.

Every screen should have generous spacing.

Avoid stacking components too closely together.

Prefer:

Fewer elements with more space.

Rather than:

Many elements competing for attention.

---

# Layout Philosophy

Layouts should feel predictable.

Most pages follow this rhythm:

Header

↓

Primary Action

↓

Main Content

↓

Supporting Content

↓

Helpful Details

Users should immediately understand where they are.

---

# Visual Hierarchy

Each page should have one primary focus.

Not three.

Not five.

One.

Supporting content should fade into the background.

Use typography, spacing, and scale.

Not excessive color.

---

# Typography Philosophy

Typography should communicate calm confidence.

Text should never feel decorative.

Headings should be strong without shouting.

Body text should feel effortless to read.

The hierarchy should be obvious even without color.

Typography should create rhythm throughout the interface.

---

# Color Philosophy

Color supports emotion.

It should never overwhelm.

The palette should feel warm and timeless.

Avoid harsh saturation.

Avoid neon colors.

Avoid large areas of bright color.

Neutral backgrounds should dominate.

Accent colors should guide attention.

Never decorate with color.

Use color intentionally.

---

# Surface Design

The interface should rely on layered surfaces.

Background

↓

Section

↓

Card

↓

Interactive Element

Each layer should feel distinct while remaining subtle.

Avoid excessive contrast.

---

# Shadows

Shadows should communicate depth.

Not drama.

Use soft shadows.

Small blur.

Low opacity.

Avoid dramatic floating elements.

Avoid heavy drop shadows.

---

# Borders

Borders should be subtle.

They exist to organize.

Not decorate.

Use borders sparingly.

When whitespace can separate content, prefer whitespace.

---

# Corner Radius

Rounded corners communicate warmth.

Every component should feel approachable.

Avoid sharp edges.

Avoid exaggerated rounding.

The interface should feel refined.

---

# Icons

Icons should support comprehension.

Not replace words.

Every icon should have a purpose.

Icons should be:

Simple

Consistent

Recognizable

Minimal

Avoid decorative icons.

Avoid novelty icons.

---

# Illustrations

Illustrations should reinforce humanity.

They should tell stories.

Not explain software.

Illustrations should feel:

Warm

Friendly

Timeless

Optimistic

Never childish.

Never cartoonish.

---

## Dave

Dave is the homepage mascot.

Dave represents the feeling of forgetting someone important.

He introduces the problem.

Once onboarding begins, relationships become the hero.

Dave gradually disappears from the experience.

He should never distract from the user's important people.

---

# Photography

Photography should feel authentic.

Use real moments.

Real people.

Real emotion.

Avoid obvious stock photography.

Avoid staged office environments.

Avoid technology imagery.

Relationships are always more important than devices.

---

# Motion Philosophy

Motion should feel invisible.

Animations should explain.

Not entertain.

Movement should help users understand:

Where they came from.

Where they are going.

What just happened.

Animations should never delay the user.

---

# Animation Principles

Animations should be:

Fast

Smooth

Purposeful

Natural

Avoid bouncing.

Avoid spinning.

Avoid flashy effects.

Avoid unnecessary movement.

Every animation should answer:

Why is this moving?

---

# Dashboard Visual Language

The dashboard should feel like opening a thoughtful planner.

Not a business analytics dashboard.

Large welcoming hero.

Upcoming moments.

Relationship highlights.

Helpful suggestions.

Minimal statistics.

Relationships first.

Numbers second.

---

# Relationship Pages

Relationship pages should resemble a living scrapbook.

Timeline first.

Stories.

Memories.

Photos.

Important details.

Cards sent.

Relationship health.

Everything should reinforce one idea:

This relationship has a history.

---

# Card Creation

Writing a card should feel creative.

Not transactional.

Remove distractions.

Keep focus on:

Recipient

Occasion

Draft

Memories

Editing

The interface should disappear while writing.

---

# Forms

Forms should never feel like paperwork.

Questions should feel conversational.

One clear task at a time.

Large tap targets.

Clear labels.

Helpful examples.

Minimal required fields.

---

# Lists

Lists should scan effortlessly.

Strong titles.

Helpful metadata.

Generous spacing.

Consistent alignment.

Avoid dense tables when possible.

---

# Tables

Tables should only exist when comparison matters.

Business features may require them.

Personal experiences should rarely use tables.

Whenever possible, transform data into cards.

---

# Navigation

Navigation should quietly guide users.

It should never dominate the page.

Primary navigation should contain only essential destinations.

Everything else belongs deeper within the experience.

---

# Business Experience

Business users deserve the same warmth.

Professional does not mean cold.

Business screens should still feel human.

Relationships remain the focus.

Not sales pipelines.

Not customer databases.

---

# Mobile Design

Mobile should prioritize one thing at a time.

One primary action.

One clear focus.

Minimal clutter.

Thumb friendly interactions.

Large spacing.

Large buttons.

---

# Desktop Design

Desktop should use additional space thoughtfully.

Never stretch content edge to edge.

Use whitespace generously.

Content should remain readable.

Avoid ultra wide layouts.

---

# Accessibility

Accessibility improves the experience for everyone.

Use sufficient contrast.

Readable typography.

Large touch targets.

Visible focus states.

Clear labels.

Meaningful icon labels.

Never rely solely on color.

---

# Dark Mode Philosophy

Dark mode should feel elegant.

Not futuristic.

Use soft dark neutrals.

Avoid pure black.

Maintain warmth.

Maintain readability.

Avoid glowing colors.

---

# Visual Consistency

Every screen should appear to belong to the same family.

Spacing.

Typography.

Colors.

Components.

Interactions.

Motion.

Everything should feel unified.

Users should never wonder if they entered another application.

---

# Things To Avoid

Avoid clutter.

Avoid visual noise.

Avoid excessive gradients.

Avoid glassmorphism.

Avoid unnecessary charts.

Avoid dashboards full of metrics.

Avoid AI themed graphics.

Avoid robots.

Avoid gears.

Avoid abstract technology imagery.

Avoid generic productivity app aesthetics.

Avoid enterprise software styling.

Avoid excessive shadows.

Avoid tiny text.

Avoid crowded forms.

Avoid decorative animations.

Avoid dark patterns.

Avoid flashy marketing sections inside the application.

---

# Examples Of Good Visual Decisions

Instead of six KPI cards, show one thoughtful reminder.

Instead of a complex analytics dashboard, show upcoming people who matter.

Instead of bright warning colors, use calm guidance.

Instead of large promotional banners, highlight meaningful relationships.

Instead of overwhelming menus, present only what users need right now.

Instead of making AI the centerpiece, make people the centerpiece.

---

# Visual Consistency Checklist

Before shipping any screen, verify:

Does this feel calm?

Does this feel premium?

Does this feel trustworthy?

Does this feel human?

Is there enough whitespace?

Is there one clear primary action?

Are relationships more prominent than features?

Could anything be removed?

Would a first time user immediately understand the page?

Does this feel like software?

If yes, simplify.

Does this feel like a world class Relationship Concierge?

If yes, it belongs in F.I. Forgot.

---

# Final Principle

The best interface is one users stop noticing.

When people finish using F.I. Forgot, they should remember the person they were thinking about.

Not the software they used.

That is the visual language of F.I. Forgot.
