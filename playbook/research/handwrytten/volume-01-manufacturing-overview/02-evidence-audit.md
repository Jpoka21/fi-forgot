## Audit Methodology

This audit reviewed the full Volume 01 research report line by line and extracted material claims using the following process:

1. **Claim identification.** Every statement presented as fact, operational practice, capacity figure, timing promise, technology capability, facility detail, workflow step, product capability, competitive advantage, or limitation was cataloged. Recommendations labeled as implications for F.I. Forgot were excluded unless they restated a vendor claim. Report-labeled inferences were audited separately from facts.
2. **Source reconstruction.** For each claim, the audit recorded the source as presented in the research report. Where the report cites only a domain (`handwrytten.com`, `inc.com`, `bizjournals.com`) without a specific page title, URL path, capture date, or document version, that limitation was recorded explicitly. This audit did **not** independently retrieve or re-fetch external webpages. No claim supported only by a domain-level citation in the source report is described as independently verified. Such claims are evaluated as **supported as represented in the source report; exact source capture pending**.
3. **Classification.** Each claim received a source classification from the approved taxonomy: Verified Official Fact, Verified Through Multiple Sources, Developer Documentation, Support Documentation, Official Marketing Claim, Independent Reporting, Historical Information, Reasonable Inference, Unverified, Contradicted, or Requires Direct Vendor Confirmation.
4. **Temporal review.** Claims were marked Current, Historical, or Mixed. Historical or interview-based figures were not approved as current operating baselines.
5. **Support test.** The audit asked whether the cited source category, as described in the report, supports the exact wording used **as represented in the research report**. **Directly Supported: Yes** is reserved for claims where the source report identifies a sufficiently exact page or document (for example, guarantee page, scheduling guide, signature guide, API v3.15.0) and clearly maps the claim to that source. Domain-only citations remain **Partial** support with traceability limitation. Developer and API evidence was not treated as proof of universal production usage. Marketing language was not promoted to verified manufacturing fact.
6. **Outcome assignment.** Each claim received one of four outcomes: APPROVED, APPROVED WITH LIMITATION, HOLD, or REJECTED, using the rules in Audit Standards below.
7. **Grouping.** Closely related statements sharing the same source, evidence level, temporal scope, and audit outcome were combined into one register row where appropriate. The register is comprehensive for material claims but not sentence-by-sentence.

## Audit Standards



### APPROVED

Use only when a current, identifiable, reliable source described in the report supports the claim as represented and the wording does not exceed the evidence. Approval does not mean the webpage was independently retrieved in this audit pass. Claims approved here still require exact source capture before highest-confidence promotion.

### APPROVED WITH LIMITATION

Use when the core claim is supported but requires narrower wording, date qualification, scope limitation, source qualification, or explicit distinction between capability, promise, and measured performance.

### HOLD

Use when the claim may be true but current evidence in the report is insufficient, ambiguous, promotional, outdated, interview-based, inferred, or dependent on a single weak source.

### REJECTED

Use when the claim is contradicted within the report, unsupported, materially overstated, improperly inferred, or unsuitable for any verified-facts promotion without replacement wording.

### Major reliability strengths

- The research report consistently distinguishes official facts, support documentation, developer documentation, marketing claims, independent reporting, inference, and unverified gaps.
- Core production identity claims—real-pen robotic writing, envelope addressing, Arizona operating address, API/platform workflow breadth, and guarantee boundaries—are supported by multiple official source types named in the report.
- The report explicitly flags timing-language tension, pricing drift, fleet-growth chronology, and Phoenix/Tempe wording differences rather than hiding them.
- Manufacturing opacity is acknowledged; the report does not falsely claim visibility into print equipment, QA thresholds, or disaster recovery.



### Major reliability weaknesses

- Most official citations are domain-level (`handwrytten.com`) without page URLs, titles, or capture snapshots in the report body, limiting independent re-verification from this repository alone.
- Several important figures (daily throughput, handwriting-style count, fleet utilization) rely on interview reporting or marketing-adjacent pages.
- Current official timing statements are not fully harmonized across guarantee, FAQ, and scheduling guide sources as cited.
- Competitor-comparison pages are used in the report for some operational claims that lack neutral corroboration.



### Major contradictions

No claim was found to be flatly contradicted by reliable evidence inside the report. The main conflicts are **scope or date drift**, not factual impossibility:

- Production timing language varies across current official sources (1 business day vs 1–2 vs 1–3).
- Retail card pricing differs between an FAQ reference ($3.25) and current pricing page ($3.75).
- Robot fleet counts differ by year (115 historical official, ~185 independent 2025, 200 current official).
- Phoenix postmark wording vs Tempe street address reflects metro labeling, not facility contradiction.



### Major source gaps

- Exact print production process before handwriting
- Pen and ink specifications
- QA criteria, rejection rates, and rework loops
- Return-mail and undeliverable handling
- Disaster recovery and backup-facility design
- Customer-order fulfillment split between Arizona and 3PL sites
- Current official handwriting-style count
- Whether postcards are a standard catalog product

## Final Audit Conclusion

The Volume 01 research report is **sufficiently reliable to support a Verified Facts Library**, but only for a **narrow, well-qualified subset** of claims. The report’s greatest value is not breadth of manufacturing transparency—it is disciplined separation between what Handwrytten officially discloses about customer workflow, platform capability, robotic writing method, guarantee boundaries, and Arizona-centered operations versus what remains marketing, inference, or unknown.

### Strong enough for standardization now

- Real-pen robotic writing and envelope addressing
- Documented customer, bulk, and API order workflows
- Guarantee and cancellation boundaries
- Arizona operating address and postal-origin disclosures
- Product add-ons (inserts, gift cards, signatures, QR) at platform level
- Official fleet-size and per-robot capacity language, with utilization excluded



### Too uncertain for manufacturing standardization

- Internal print production path
- Pen/ink specification
- QA thresholds, defect rates, and rework
- Actual throughput and peak capacity
- Fulfillment-network design (Arizona vs 3PL)
- Business continuity and return-mail handling
- Marketing realism and comparative superiority claims



### Direct Handwrytten diligence required

**Yes.** Direct vendor diligence is required before finalizing any F.I. Forgot Manufacturing and Production Standard. Public research in Volume 01 establishes that Handwrytten is a credible, workflow-rich robotic mail platform, but it does **not** disclose enough internal plant detail to support deterministic manufacturing rules, quality thresholds, or continuity assumptions without vendor confirmation.
