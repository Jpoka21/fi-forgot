**Standard statement:** F.I. Forgot maintains **one authoritative Typography** standard that defines how typography-domain decisions are governed, how typographic element rules are evaluated, and how Layer 3 typography operates within philosophical and brand authority without absorbing composition, handwritten message, surface layout, or implementation behavior. This standard populates Layer 3 — Visual Element Systems for the typography element domain. It does not define font families, font sizes, typographic scales, hierarchy systems, accessibility thresholds, template geometry, or engineering implementation.

**Source basis:** Company judgment per `FI-DSN-GOV-003`. This standard is not derived from vendor facts, verified evidence, or Brain runtime behavior.

---

## 1. Purpose

This standard is the **normative governing foundation** for F.I. Forgot Volume 02 Layer 3 — Typography (`CLS-TYP`).

It translates approved Layer 3 typography architecture into enduring typography-domain commitments and judgment discipline that downstream standards in Composition, Visual Boundaries, and peer volumes must remain reconcilable with.

This standard does not replace frozen Volume 02 Visual Design Architecture, `FI-DSN-PRN-001`, or `FI-DSN-STD-001`. It operates within that architecture and remains subordinate to Layer 1 philosophical authority and Layer 2 brand authority.

## 5. Purpose Architecture

Typography governs the **typography element domain** as a reusable visual building block within F.I. Forgot governed visuals. It defines what typographic decisions belong to `CLS-TYP` and what permanent discipline governs those decisions — without becoming a font catalog, hierarchy specification, or implementation guide.

### 5.1 Typography-domain authority

A decision belongs to the typography domain when typographic character, usage, or system behavior is the **principal normative subject**.

**Placement discipline** — authors SHALL apply the following test to determine whether `CLS-TYP` is the appropriate principal classification. This test operationalizes `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1; it does not replace principal-subject law.

| Step | Question | If yes → |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Is typographic character, usage, or system behavior the principal normative subject? | Candidate for `CLS-TYP` / Layer 3 Typography |
| 2 | Would the normative answer change if F.I. Forgot brand identity were removed from the decision? | Layer 2 — `CLS-BEX` when identity-bound expression is principal |
| 3 | Is relational discipline among elements — hierarchy, balance, prominence, density — the principal subject? | Layer 4 — `CLS-CMP` when relational |
| 4 | Is handwritten message presentation the principal subject? | Manufacturing / message-engine — not `CLS-TYP` |
| 5 | Is surface layout, template regions, or coordinates the principal subject? | Volume 03 |
| 6 | Is a global prohibition or accessibility minimum the principal subject regardless of typographic intent? | Layer 5 |

When steps conflict, **principal normative subject** per `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1 governs.

Typography owns **type behavior** as an element domain. It does not own art-vs-text prominence, cross-element hierarchy, or relational discipline among typographic and non-typographic elements per frozen Volume 02 §12.1.

### 5.2 Typography-domain discipline

Typography-domain discipline governs two enduring facets as one constitutional subject:

| Facet | Governing role |
|-------|----------------|
| **Typographic system character** | What typographic treatment may be as a reusable element system — enduring character and usage categories, not specific font names or measurements |
| **Element boundaries** | What typography as an element domain may and may not do — permitted, required, and prohibited typographic treatment distinct from brand boundaries and Layer 5 global exclusions |

Typography-domain discipline is **not** brand boundary discipline. A typographic rule may comply with element-domain discipline while requiring separate brand reconcilability evaluation. Typography-domain discipline is **not** Layer 5 global exclusion.

### 5.3 Normative requirements — Purpose Architecture

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|

| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R07` | Typography SHALL govern typography-domain discipline — reusable typographic character, usage boundaries, and element-level permitted and prohibited treatment. | Company judgment |

## 6. Decision Architecture

### 6.1 Stacked evaluation procedure

Typography decisions SHALL be evaluated through a **stacked evaluation procedure**. Each gate is sequential. Failure at any gate renders the decision unsuccessful at the Typography layer regardless of outcomes at later gates.

| Gate | Evaluation discipline |
|------|----------------------|
| **1 — Constitutional** | Compliance with Volume 02 P1–P11; no contradiction with inherited architectural law |
| **2 — Layer 5** | Compliance with visual exclusions, accessibility foundations, and library-level quality foundations where implicated |
| **3 — Upstream reconcilability** | Apply frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` (philosophical reconcilability), then frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001` (brand reconcilability); upstream standards SHALL NOT be redefined here |
| **4 — Typography-appropriateness** | Compliance with typography-domain authority and typography-domain discipline |
| **5 — Layer 3 scope** | The decision stays within Layer 3 typography authority and does not govern composition, handwritten message, surface layout, contextual selection, Brain behavior, or implementation |

A treatment that passes typography-appropriateness but fails upstream reconcilability is unsuccessful. A treatment that passes upstream reconcilability but fails typography-appropriateness is unsuccessful. A treatment that passes both may still fail if it exceeds Layer 3 authority or violates an upstream gate.

#### Normative requirements — Stacked evaluation

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R08` | Typography decisions SHALL be evaluated through the stacked evaluation procedure in Section 6.1. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R09` | Constitutional and Layer 5 gates SHALL be applied before typographic preference or typography-appropriateness evaluation. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R10` | Upstream reconcilability SHALL be evaluated by applying frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` and then frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001`; this standard SHALL NOT redefine Layer 1 or Layer 2 evaluation architecture. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R11` | Typography-appropriateness SHALL be required for typography success in addition to, not instead of, upstream reconcilability. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R12` | Typography SHALL NOT govern compositional hierarchy, message-vs-art prominence, relational discipline among elements, handwritten message presentation, surface layout, contextual selection policy, Brain behavior, or implementation requirements. A typography decision that does so SHALL fail stacked evaluation at the Layer 3 scope gate. | Company judgment |

### 6.2 Judgment procedure

Authors of typography rules and typography decisions SHALL apply the following judgment discipline.

#### Judgment procedure

1. **Confirm principal subject** — apply the placement test (Section 5.1); confirm `CLS-TYP` ownership or defer to the correct layer or volume
2. **State the typography-domain question** — identify what typographic character, usage, or boundary is being decided
3. **Apply stacked evaluation** — execute Section 6.1 gates in order
4. **Record downstream impact** — identify which Layer 4, Volume 03, or peer volume standards are constrained
5. **Record reconcilability** — demonstrate reconcilability at freeze review per `FI-DSN-GOV-001` §7.2 and record material dependencies in `FI-DSN-REG-001` where applicable

#### Enduring judgment principles

These principles express Layer 3 typography reasoning discipline. They do not author domain-specific rules or typographic specifications.

| Principle | Guidance |
|-----------|----------|
| **Domain before decoration** | If the normative answer depends on typographic character or usage as an element, the decision is typography-domain — not compositional or implementational |
| **Type behavior before placement** | Typography governs what type may be; it does not govern where type is placed on a surface |
| **Message presentation is out of scope** | Handwritten message presentation is not a typography-domain subject for this product |

#### Normative requirements — Judgment procedure

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R13` | Every typography decision SHALL confirm `CLS-TYP` ownership through the placement test before approval. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R14` | Every typography decision SHALL complete the judgment procedure in Section 6.2 before approval. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R15` | Future Volume 02 standards and peer-volume standards constrained by Layer 3 typography SHALL demonstrate reconcilability with this standard during individual freeze review per `FI-DSN-GOV-001` §7.2. Governed revision to typography-domain rules in this standard SHALL trigger reconcilability assessment for materially affected downstream standards recorded in `FI-DSN-REG-001` **Dependencies** per `FI-DSN-GOV-001` §15. | Company judgment |

### 6.3 Conflict reconciliation

Competing typography goals are permanent. Typography governance does not eliminate tension. It governs how tensions are resolved.

When normative typography goals conflict, authors SHALL apply the following procedure after identifying competing principal subjects per `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1:

1. Apply Constitutional Inheritance — prohibited treatments remain prohibited regardless of typographic preference
2. Apply Layer 5 authority where visual exclusion, accessibility foundations, or safety-related visual boundaries are implicated
3. Apply upstream reconcilability per frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` and frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001`, including their conflict reconciliation procedures where upstream goals are in tension
4. Apply typography-appropriateness and typography-domain discipline
5. If unresolved — escalate through governed revision, REG reconciliation, or architecture review. Silent domain override is prohibited.

No typographic quality automatically wins every conflict without passing the stacked evaluation gates.

*Illustrative note (non-normative):* Authors may document specific tensions encountered during standard drafting. Such documentation does not create a permanent conflict taxonomy. Resolution follows the procedure above.

#### Normative requirements — Conflict reconciliation

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R16` | When normative typography goals conflict, authors SHALL apply the conflict reconciliation procedure in Section 6.3 before approval. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R17` | Conflict resolution SHALL apply Layer 5 authority and upstream reconcilability before typography-specific convenience. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R18` | No typographic quality SHALL automatically prevail in every conflict without stacked evaluation. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-002-R19` | Unresolved typography conflicts SHALL NOT be settled by silent override. Governed escalation is required. | Company judgment |

## 7. Company Judgment

This standard is entirely based on company judgment.

F.I. Forgot has chosen to govern typography as a distinct Layer 3 element domain because philosophical and brand authority alone cannot determine what typographic treatment may be as a reusable visual building block. That separation is not derived from vendor documentation, verified manufacturing facts, or Brain runtime behavior.

`FI-DSN-PRN-001` and `FI-DSN-STD-001` informed drafting as frozen upstream authority. Layer 1 and Layer 2 requirements are applied, not re-authored, unless explicitly incorporated here as Layer 3 requirements.
