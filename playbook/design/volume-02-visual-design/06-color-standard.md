**Standard statement:** F.I. Forgot maintains **one authoritative Color** standard that defines how color-domain decisions are governed, how color element rules are evaluated, and how Layer 3 color operates within philosophical and brand authority without absorbing typography, composition, handwritten message, surface layout, or implementation behavior. This standard populates Layer 3 — Visual Element Systems for the color element domain. It does not define palette values, color specifications, accessibility numeric thresholds, template geometry, manufacturing color calibration, or engineering implementation.

**Source basis:** Company judgment per `FI-DSN-GOV-003`. This standard is not derived from vendor facts, verified evidence, or Brain runtime behavior.

---

## 1. Purpose

This standard is the **normative governing foundation** for F.I. Forgot Volume 02 Layer 3 — Color (`CLS-COL`).

It translates approved Layer 3 color architecture into enduring color-domain commitments and judgment discipline that downstream standards in Composition, Visual Boundaries, and peer volumes must remain reconcilable with.

This standard does not replace frozen Volume 02 Visual Design Architecture, `FI-DSN-PRN-001`, or `FI-DSN-STD-001`. It operates within that architecture and remains subordinate to Layer 1 philosophical authority and Layer 2 brand authority.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Color-domain decision** | A governed visual decision whose principal normative subject is color character, color usage, color role, or color system behavior for governed visuals — classified under `CLS-COL` when color is principal |
| **Color-domain discipline** | Permanent governance of reusable color character, color role boundaries, and element-level permitted and prohibited color treatment |
| **Color role** | The governed functional or expressive purpose a color treatment serves within a visual composition at the element-domain level — distinct from relational hierarchy among elements |
| **Color-appropriateness** | Whether a governed visual decision complies with color-domain authority and discipline while remaining philosophically and brand reconcilable with upstream standards |
| **Upstream reconcilability** | Consistency with frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` and `FI-DSN-STD-001` — applied, not redefined, by this standard |
| **Constitutional inheritance** | Non-negotiable authority inherited from frozen Volume 02 Visual Design Architecture P1–P11 and reconcilability obligations inherited from upstream Layer 1 and Layer 2 standards |

## 5. Purpose Architecture

Color governs the **color element domain** as a reusable visual building block within F.I. Forgot governed visuals. It defines what color decisions belong to `CLS-COL` and what permanent discipline governs those decisions — without becoming a palette specification, surface color implementation guide, or manufacturing calibration standard.

### 5.1 Color-domain authority

A decision belongs to the color domain when color character, color usage, color role, or color system behavior is the **principal normative subject**.

**Placement discipline** — authors SHALL apply the following test to determine whether `CLS-COL` is the appropriate principal classification. This test operationalizes `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1; it does not replace principal-subject law.

| Step | Question | If yes → |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Is color character, color usage, color role, or color system behavior the principal normative subject? | Candidate for `CLS-COL` / Layer 3 Color |
| 2 | Would the normative answer change if F.I. Forgot brand identity were removed from the decision? | Layer 2 — `CLS-BEX` when identity-bound expression is principal |
| 3 | Is relational discipline among elements — hierarchy, balance, prominence, density — the principal subject? | Layer 4 — `CLS-CMP` when relational |
| 4 | Is handwritten message presentation the principal subject? | Manufacturing / message-engine — not `CLS-COL` |
| 5 | Is surface layout, template regions, or coordinates the principal subject? | Volume 03 |
| 6 | Is a global prohibition or accessibility minimum the principal subject regardless of color intent? | Layer 5 |

When steps conflict, **principal normative subject** per `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1 governs.

Color owns **color behavior** as an element domain. It does not own art-vs-text prominence, cross-element relational hierarchy, or typographic character per frozen Volume 02 §12.1.

### 5.2 Color-domain discipline

Color-domain discipline governs three enduring facets as one constitutional subject:

| Facet | Governing role |
|-------|----------------|
| **Color system character** | What color treatment may be as a reusable element system — enduring color roles and usage categories, not specific palette values or color specifications |
| **Color role and hierarchy** | How color functions within governed visuals at the element-domain level — role assignment and color-to-color precedence within the color domain, not cross-element compositional hierarchy |
| **Element boundaries** | What color as an element domain may and may not do — permitted, required, and prohibited color treatment distinct from brand boundaries and Layer 5 global exclusions |

Color-domain discipline is **not** brand boundary discipline. A color rule may comply with element-domain discipline while requiring separate brand reconcilability evaluation. Color-domain discipline is **not** Layer 5 global exclusion.

### 5.3 Normative requirements — Purpose Architecture

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|

| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R07` | Color SHALL govern color-domain discipline — reusable color character, color role boundaries, and element-level permitted and prohibited treatment. | Company judgment |

## 6. Decision Architecture

### 6.1 Stacked evaluation procedure

Color decisions SHALL be evaluated through a **stacked evaluation procedure**. Each gate is sequential. Failure at any gate renders the decision unsuccessful at the Color layer regardless of outcomes at later gates.

| Gate | Evaluation discipline |
|------|----------------------|
| **1 — Constitutional** | Compliance with Volume 02 P1–P11; no contradiction with inherited architectural law |
| **2 — Layer 5** | Compliance with visual exclusions, accessibility foundations, and library-level quality foundations where implicated |
| **3 — Upstream reconcilability** | Apply frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` (philosophical reconcilability), then frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001` (brand reconcilability); upstream standards SHALL NOT be redefined here |
| **4 — Color-appropriateness** | Compliance with color-domain authority and color-domain discipline |
| **5 — Layer 3 scope** | The decision stays within Layer 3 color authority and does not govern composition, typography, handwritten message, surface layout, contextual selection, Brain behavior, or implementation |

A treatment that passes color-appropriateness but fails upstream reconcilability is unsuccessful. A treatment that passes upstream reconcilability but fails color-appropriateness is unsuccessful. A treatment that passes both may still fail if it exceeds Layer 3 authority or violates an upstream gate.

#### Normative requirements — Stacked evaluation

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R08` | Color decisions SHALL be evaluated through the stacked evaluation procedure in Section 6.1. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R09` | Constitutional and Layer 5 gates SHALL be applied before color preference or color-appropriateness evaluation. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R10` | Upstream reconcilability SHALL be evaluated by applying frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` and then frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001`; this standard SHALL NOT redefine Layer 1 or Layer 2 evaluation architecture. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R11` | Color-appropriateness SHALL be required for color success in addition to, not instead of, upstream reconcilability. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R12` | Color SHALL NOT govern compositional hierarchy, message-vs-art prominence, relational discipline among elements, typographic character, handwritten message presentation, surface layout, contextual selection policy, Brain behavior, or implementation requirements. A color decision that does so SHALL fail stacked evaluation at the Layer 3 scope gate. | Company judgment |

### 6.2 Judgment procedure

Authors of color rules and color decisions SHALL apply the following judgment discipline.

#### Judgment procedure

1. **Confirm principal subject** — apply the placement test (Section 5.1); confirm `CLS-COL` ownership or defer to the correct layer or volume
2. **State the color-domain question** — identify what color character, color role, usage, or boundary is being decided
3. **Apply stacked evaluation** — execute Section 6.1 gates in order
4. **Record downstream impact** — identify which Layer 4, Volume 03, or peer volume standards are constrained
5. **Record reconcilability** — demonstrate reconcilability at freeze review per `FI-DSN-GOV-001` §7.2 and record material dependencies in `FI-DSN-REG-001` where applicable

#### Enduring judgment principles

These principles express Layer 3 color reasoning discipline. They do not author domain-specific rules or color specifications.

| Principle | Guidance |
|-----------|----------|
| **Domain before decoration** | If the normative answer depends on color character or usage as an element, the decision is color-domain — not compositional or implementational |
| **Role before placement** | Color governs what color may be and what role it may serve; it does not govern where color is placed on a surface |
| **Intentionality before palette** | Color governance governs decision discipline and role boundaries; it does not prescribe specific color values |

#### Normative requirements — Judgment procedure

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R13` | Every color decision SHALL confirm `CLS-COL` ownership through the placement test before approval. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R14` | Every color decision SHALL complete the judgment procedure in Section 6.2 before approval. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R15` | Future Volume 02 standards and peer-volume standards constrained by Layer 3 color SHALL demonstrate reconcilability with this standard during individual freeze review per `FI-DSN-GOV-001` §7.2. Governed revision to color-domain rules in this standard SHALL trigger reconcilability assessment for materially affected downstream standards recorded in `FI-DSN-REG-001` **Dependencies** per `FI-DSN-GOV-001` §15. | Company judgment |

### 6.3 Conflict reconciliation

Competing color goals are permanent. Color governance does not eliminate tension. It governs how tensions are resolved.

When normative color goals conflict, authors SHALL apply the following procedure after identifying competing principal subjects per `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1:

1. Apply Constitutional Inheritance — prohibited treatments remain prohibited regardless of color preference
2. Apply Layer 5 authority where visual exclusion, accessibility foundations, or safety-related visual boundaries are implicated
3. Apply upstream reconcilability per frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` and frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001`, including their conflict reconciliation procedures where upstream goals are in tension
4. Apply color-appropriateness and color-domain discipline
5. If unresolved — escalate through governed revision, REG reconciliation, or architecture review. Silent domain override is prohibited.

No color quality automatically wins every conflict without passing the stacked evaluation gates.

*Illustrative note (non-normative):* Authors may document specific tensions encountered during standard drafting. Such documentation does not create a permanent conflict taxonomy. Resolution follows the procedure above.

#### Normative requirements — Conflict reconciliation

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R16` | When normative color goals conflict, authors SHALL apply the conflict reconciliation procedure in Section 6.3 before approval. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R17` | Conflict resolution SHALL apply Layer 5 authority and upstream reconcilability before color-specific convenience. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R18` | No color quality SHALL automatically prevail in every conflict without stacked evaluation. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-016-R19` | Unresolved color conflicts SHALL NOT be settled by silent override. Governed escalation is required. | Company judgment |

## 7. Company Judgment

This standard is entirely based on company judgment.

F.I. Forgot has chosen to govern color as a distinct Layer 3 element domain because philosophical and brand authority alone cannot determine what color treatment may be as a reusable visual building block. That separation is not derived from vendor documentation, verified manufacturing facts, or Brain runtime behavior.

`FI-DSN-PRN-001` and `FI-DSN-STD-001` informed drafting as frozen upstream authority. Layer 1 and Layer 2 requirements are applied, not re-authored, unless explicitly incorporated here as Layer 3 requirements.
