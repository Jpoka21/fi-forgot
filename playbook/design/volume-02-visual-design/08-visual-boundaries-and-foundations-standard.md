**Standard statement:** F.I. Forgot maintains **one authoritative Visual Boundaries and Foundations** standard that defines how design-language-level visual exclusions, accessibility foundations, and visual quality foundations are governed; how boundary and foundation failures are evaluated; and how Layer 5 authority prevails over lower-layer preference without absorbing element-domain ownership, composition ownership, surface implementation, contextual artwork intelligence, or engineering accessibility law. This standard populates Layer 5 — Visual Boundaries and Foundations for cross-cutting constraint and foundation law. It does not define WCAG numeric thresholds, UI component behavior, template geometry, manufacturing inspection procedures, artwork selection, fixed palettes, or any requirement that Dave imagery appear on card surfaces.

**Source basis:** Company judgment per `FI-DSN-GOV-003`. This standard is not derived from vendor facts, verified evidence, or Brain runtime behavior.

---

## 1. Purpose

This standard is the **normative governing foundation** for F.I. Forgot Volume 02 Layer 5 — Visual Boundaries and Foundations (`CLS-VQA` principal; `CLS-ACI` secondary where accessibility foundations are implicated).

It translates approved Layer 5 architecture into enduring exclusion, accessibility foundation, and visual quality foundation commitments that downstream volumes and peer standards must remain reconcilable with.

This standard does not replace frozen Volume 02 Visual Design Architecture, `FI-DSN-PRN-001`, `FI-DSN-STD-001`, or frozen Layer 3 and Layer 4 standards. It operates as the terminal design-language constraint layer and remains reconcilable with upstream philosophical and brand authority without re-authoring them.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Boundary-domain decision** | A governed visual decision whose principal normative subject is a visual exclusion, accessibility foundation, or visual quality foundation — classified under `CLS-VQA` or `CLS-ACI` when boundary or foundation law is principal |
| **Visual exclusion** | A design-language-level prohibition or rejection condition that makes an otherwise plausible visual treatment constitutionally unacceptable |
| **Accessibility foundation** | A minimum inclusion boundary for visual presentation at design-language level — distinct from implementation-specific accessibility engineering |
| **Visual quality foundation** | A library-level cross-standard quality floor governing systemic coherence, integrity, and controlled complexity — distinct from per-standard Validation sections and production QA |
| **Foundation failure** | A condition in which a governed visual presentation fails a required accessibility or visual quality foundation established by this standard |
| **Combined-system failure** | A condition in which visual treatments that may be individually reconcilable produce an unacceptable result when evaluated as one governed visual system |
| **Layer 5 override** | The constitutional precedence by which visual exclusions and foundation failures prevail over lower-layer preference per frozen Volume 02 §7.2 |
| **Upstream reconcilability** | Consistency with frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` and `FI-DSN-STD-001` — applied, not redefined, by this standard |
| **Constitutional inheritance** | Non-negotiable authority inherited from frozen Volume 02 Visual Design Architecture P1–P11 and reconcilability obligations inherited from upstream Layer 1 and Layer 2 standards |

## 5. Purpose Architecture

Layer 5 governs **cross-cutting visual constraints** that are not element definitions. It defines when visually plausible choices must be rejected despite satisfying lower-layer preferences.

### 5.1 Boundary-domain authority

A decision belongs to the boundary domain when a visual exclusion, accessibility foundation, or visual quality foundation is the **principal normative subject**.

**Placement discipline** — authors SHALL apply the following test to determine whether `CLS-VQA` or `CLS-ACI` is the appropriate principal classification. This test operationalizes `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1; it does not replace principal-subject law.

| Step | Question | If yes → |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Is a visual exclusion, accessibility foundation, or visual quality foundation the principal normative subject? | Candidate for Layer 5 — `CLS-VQA` or `CLS-ACI` |
| 2 | Is color character, usage, or role the principal subject? | Layer 3 — `CLS-COL` — `FI-DSN-STD-016` |
| 3 | Is typographic character or usage the principal subject? | Layer 3 — `CLS-TYP` — `FI-DSN-STD-002` |
| 4 | Is artwork or illustration character the principal subject? | Layer 3 — `CLS-ART` — `FI-DSN-STD-017` |
| 5 | Is relational discipline among elements the principal subject? | Layer 4 — `CLS-CMP` — `FI-DSN-STD-003` |
| 6 | Is surface layout, template regions, or coordinates the principal subject? | Volume 03 |
| 7 | Is contextual artwork selection, occasion semantics, or personalization policy the principal subject? | Volume 04 |

When steps conflict, **principal normative subject** per `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1 governs.

### 5.2 Layer 5 sub-domains

Frozen Volume 02 §6.5 organizes Layer 5 into three sub-domains. This standard governs all three as one coherent principal-subject artifact:

| Sub-domain | Governing role |
|------------|----------------|
| **Exclusions** | Prohibited treatments, anti-patterns, and rejection conditions that make otherwise plausible visuals constitutionally unacceptable |
| **Accessibility Foundations** | Minimum inclusion boundaries for visual presentation at design-language level |
| **Visual Quality Foundations** | Cross-standard visual quality floors governing coherence, integrity, and controlled complexity |

Sub-domains are organizational. They are not separate architectural layers and do not require separate standards.

### 5.3 Visual exclusion discipline

Visual exclusion discipline governs **rejection authority** at design-language level. Exclusions apply to classes of visual failure, not personal taste.

| Exclusion class | Governing role |
|-----------------|----------------|
| **Comprehension impairment** | Visual presentation that materially impairs comprehension of meaningful content |
| **Legibility and readability failure** | Visual presentation that fails foundational legibility or readability expectations at design-language level |
| **Unacceptable conflict and hierarchy defeat** | Visual conflict, defeated intended hierarchy, or misleading emphasis that undermines governed visual intent |
| **Message primacy interference** | Visual treatment that materially interferes with handwritten-message primacy per P11 |
| **Combined-system failure** | Treatments reconcilable in isolation that fail as one governed visual system |

Exclusion discipline is **not** element-domain discipline. A color, typography, or artwork rule may comply with its Layer 3 authority while still failing Layer 5 exclusion evaluation.

### 5.4 Accessibility foundation discipline

Accessibility foundation discipline governs **minimum inclusion boundaries** without becoming implementation accessibility engineering.

| Foundation facet | Governing role |
|------------------|----------------|
| **Discernibility** | Meaning-bearing elements SHALL remain sufficiently distinguishable at foundation level |
| **Distinction without color-alone reliance** | Where distinction among meaning-bearing elements is constitutionally required, meaning SHALL NOT rely on color alone |
| **Hierarchy accessibility** | Visual hierarchy SHALL remain accessible at foundation level without prescribing numeric contrast ratios or UI inspection methods |

Accessibility foundations constrain downstream design decisions. They do not replace `FI-DSN-STD-002`, `FI-DSN-STD-016`, Volume 03 surface law, or engineering accessibility specifications.

### 5.5 Visual quality foundation discipline

Visual quality foundation discipline governs **library-level quality floors** without becoming production QA or per-card inspection workflow.

| Foundation facet | Governing role |
|------------------|----------------|
| **Coherence and integrity** | Governed visual systems SHALL maintain systemic coherence and visual integrity across combined presentations |
| **Controlled complexity** | Accidental visual conflict and uncontrolled complexity that defeat governed intent are foundation failures |
| **Cross-domain consistency** | Color, typography, composition, artwork, and brand expression SHALL be evaluated as one system where combined presentation is implicated |

Visual quality foundations are **not** per-standard Validation sections inside individual standards. Those remain per `FI-DSN-TPL-001`.

## 6. Decision Architecture

### 6.1 Boundary evaluation procedure

Boundary and foundation decisions SHALL be evaluated through a **boundary evaluation procedure**. Each gate is sequential. Failure at any gate renders the presentation unsuccessful at the Layer 5 layer regardless of outcomes at later gates.

| Gate | Evaluation discipline |
|------|----------------------|
| **1 — Constitutional** | Compliance with Volume 02 P1–P11; no contradiction with inherited architectural law |
| **2 — Upstream reconcilability** | Apply frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` (philosophical reconcilability), then frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001` (brand reconcilability); upstream standards SHALL NOT be redefined here |
| **3 — Exclusion** | Compliance with visual exclusion discipline in Section 5.3; prohibited or rejection-required conditions SHALL fail |
| **4 — Accessibility foundation** | Compliance with accessibility foundation discipline in Section 5.4 where implicated |
| **5 — Visual quality foundation** | Compliance with visual quality foundation discipline in Section 5.5 where implicated |
| **6 — Layer 5 scope** | The decision stays within Layer 5 boundary authority and does not govern element character, composition ownership, surface layout, contextual selection, Brain behavior, or implementation |

A presentation that passes lower-layer evaluation but fails an exclusion or foundation gate is unsuccessful. A presentation that passes Layer 5 gates may still require separate evaluation under Layer 3, Layer 4, or downstream volume authority for non-boundary subjects.

#### Normative requirements — Boundary evaluation

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R15` | Boundary and foundation decisions SHALL be evaluated through the boundary evaluation procedure in Section 6.1. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R16` | Constitutional and upstream reconcilability gates SHALL be applied before boundary preference or lower-layer aesthetic convenience. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R17` | Exclusion and foundation gates SHALL be applied before lower-layer element, composition, or brand preference where Layer 5 authority is implicated. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R18` | Upstream reconcilability SHALL be evaluated by applying frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` and then frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001`; this standard SHALL NOT redefine Layer 1 or Layer 2 evaluation architecture. | Company judgment |

### 6.2 Judgment procedure

Authors of boundary rules and boundary decisions SHALL apply the following judgment discipline.

#### Judgment procedure

1. **Confirm principal subject** — apply the placement test (Section 5.1); confirm `CLS-VQA` / `CLS-ACI` ownership or defer to the correct layer or volume
2. **State the boundary question** — identify what exclusion, accessibility foundation, or visual quality foundation is being decided
3. **Apply boundary evaluation** — execute Section 6.1 gates in order
4. **Record downstream impact** — identify which Layer 3, Layer 4, Volume 03, Volume 04, or peer standards are constrained or delegated to
5. **Record reconcilability** — demonstrate reconcilability at freeze review per `FI-DSN-GOV-001` §7.2 and record material dependencies in `FI-DSN-REG-001` where applicable

#### Enduring judgment principles

These principles express Layer 5 boundary reasoning discipline. They do not author implementation specifications.

| Principle | Guidance |
|-----------|----------|
| **Boundary before preference** | If the normative answer depends on exclusion or foundation failure, the decision is boundary-domain — not element, compositional, or implementational |
| **System before fragment** | Layer 5 evaluates combined visual systems where isolation would hide failure |
| **Foundation before convenience** | Accessibility and quality foundations constrain preference; they do not prescribe product engineering detail |

#### Normative requirements — Judgment procedure

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R19` | Every boundary decision SHALL confirm `CLS-VQA` or `CLS-ACI` ownership through the placement test before approval. | Company judgment |

### 6.3 Conflict reconciliation and Layer 5 override

Competing visual goals are permanent. Boundary governance does not eliminate tension. It governs how tensions are resolved when exclusions or foundations are implicated.

When normative goals conflict, authors SHALL apply the following procedure after identifying competing principal subjects per `FI-DSN-VOL-001` §14.1:

1. Apply Constitutional Inheritance — prohibited treatments remain prohibited regardless of lower-layer preference
2. Apply upstream reconcilability per frozen `FI-DSN-PRN-001` and frozen `FI-DSN-STD-001`, including their conflict reconciliation procedures where upstream goals are in tension
3. Apply exclusion discipline — rejection-required conditions remain rejection-required
4. Apply accessibility foundation discipline where implicated
5. Apply visual quality foundation discipline where implicated
6. If unresolved — escalate through governed revision, REG reconciliation, or architecture review. Silent override of Layer 5 authority is prohibited.

Layer 5 override does **not** transfer ownership of another domain. It rejects or constrains outcomes that lower layers might otherwise prefer. Element, composition, and downstream volume authorities retain their domains.

*Illustrative note (non-normative):* Authors may document specific tensions encountered during standard drafting. Such documentation does not create a permanent conflict taxonomy. Resolution follows the procedure above.

#### Normative requirements — Conflict reconciliation

| Req ID | Requirement | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R20` | When normative boundary goals conflict, authors SHALL apply the conflict reconciliation procedure in Section 6.3 before approval. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R21` | Conflict resolution SHALL apply exclusion and foundation authority before lower-layer aesthetic, element, or compositional convenience. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R22` | No lower-layer quality SHALL automatically prevail over an established exclusion or foundation failure without boundary evaluation. | Company judgment |
| `FI-DSN-STD-018-R23` | Unresolved boundary conflicts SHALL NOT be settled by silent override. Governed escalation is required. | Company judgment |

## 7. Company Judgment

This standard is entirely based on company judgment.

F.I. Forgot has chosen to govern visual exclusions, accessibility foundations, and visual quality foundations as one Layer 5 authority because lower layers alone cannot determine when visually plausible choices must still be rejected. That separation is not derived from vendor documentation, verified manufacturing facts, or Brain runtime behavior.

`FI-DSN-PRN-001`, `FI-DSN-STD-001`, and frozen Layer 3 and Layer 4 standards informed drafting as upstream and peer authority. Their requirements are applied or referenced, not re-authored, unless explicitly incorporated here as Layer 5 requirements.
