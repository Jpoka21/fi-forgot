# Copy and Content System

## Purpose

The Copy and Content System defines how F.I. Forgot speaks.

Every word in the product should reinforce the feeling of a premium Relationship Concierge.

This document governs all user facing language across the redesigned frontend, including headlines, buttons, labels, empty states, errors, success messages, loading states, notifications, onboarding, card creation, search, settings, billing, and AI Concierge surfaces.

The goal is consistency.

Users should never feel like different parts of the product were written by different teams.

F.I. Forgot should sound calm, thoughtful, warm, premium, and trustworthy everywhere.

This specification preserves all existing backend behavior, business logic, AI pipelines, authentication, Stripe integration, Handwrytten integration, database schema, and API contracts.

The frontend may revise user facing copy, labels, descriptions, helper text, and guidance language as long as existing functionality remains unchanged.

---

# Philosophy

F.I. Forgot is not a greeting card app.

It is not a reminder app.

It is not an AI writing tool.

It is a Relationship Concierge.

The copy must reflect that.

The product does not help users manage tasks.

It helps users care better.

The product does not help users send paper.

It helps users show up for people.

The product does not help users automate emotion.

It helps users preserve thoughtfulness when life gets busy.

Every sentence should answer:

Does this make the user feel more supported, more thoughtful, or more confident?

If not, rewrite it.

---

The F.I. Forgot voice is:

Warm.

Calm.

Thoughtful.

Premium.

Clear.

Human.

Emotionally intelligent.

Respectful.

The voice should feel like a trusted concierge speaking privately to a client.

Not a marketer.

Not a chatbot.

Not a productivity coach.

Not a greeting card company.

Not a generic SaaS app.

# Tone Guidelines

The tone should adapt by context while preserving the same core voice.

## Default Tone

Calm, helpful, and concise.

Example:

Your next card is ready to review.

## Celebration Tone

Warm and quietly positive.

Example:

That card is ready. A thoughtful note is on its way.

## Error Tone

Reassuring and clear.

Example:

We could not save that change. Nothing was lost. Please try again.

## Billing Tone

Transparent and factual.

Example:

Your current plan renews on July 18.

## Sensitive Relationship Tone

Gentle and respectful.

Example:

A thoughtful follow up may mean a lot right now.

## AI Concierge Tone

Helpful, observant, and humble.

Example:

This memory may help make the card more personal.

---

# Writing Principles

## Principle One

Write for people, not workflows.

Avoid language that makes the user feel like they are operating software.

Instead of:

Manage recipient records.

Use:

Keep track of the people who matter.

---

## Principle Two

Use simple words.

Do not use complex words to create a premium feeling.

Premium means calm and clear.

Not fancy.

---

## Principle Three

Be specific.

Instead of:

Action required.

Use:

This card needs your review before it can be mailed.

---

## Principle Four

Avoid guilt.

The product should never shame users for forgetting, delaying, or missing something.

Instead of:

You forgot Sarah's birthday.

Use:

Sarah's birthday has passed. A thoughtful belated card can still mean a lot.

---

## Principle Five

Keep users in control.

Avoid language that implies the system is acting without them.

Instead of:

We will send this card automatically.

Use:

Autopilot can prepare this card for your review.

---

## Principle Six

Do not overstate AI.

Avoid magical language.

Avoid pretending the system knows more than it does.

Instead of:

We understand your relationship perfectly.

Use:

The more memories you save, the more personal future cards can become.

---

## Principle Seven

Make next steps obvious.

Every major message should help the user understand what to do next.

# Relationship First Language

The product should organize language around people and relationships.

Prefer:

Your people.

Someone important.

The people who matter.

This relationship.

A thoughtful note.

A memory worth saving.

An upcoming occasion.

Avoid:

Contacts.

Recipients, unless needed for clarity.

Users.

Records.

Entries.

Data points.

Tasks.

Items.

Database.

---

# Concierge Language

Concierge language should feel attentive but not formal.

Preferred phrases:

I found something that may help.

This might be worth remembering.

You may want to review this.

Everything looks up to date.

This card is ready for you.

You are still in control.

This could make the card feel more personal.

Avoid:

AI recommends.

System detected.

Generated output.

Workflow complete.

Optimization suggestion.

Entity updated.

Operation successful.

---

# Headlines

Headlines should be short, clear, and human.

They should describe the value or situation.

Good examples:

Your people, thoughtfully organized.

A birthday is coming up.

This card is ready to review.

No memories yet.

Everything looks up to date.

Avoid:

Recipient Management.

Card Workflow Status.

Notification Center.

Search Results Empty.

---

# Subheadings

Subheadings should explain the page or section in one calm sentence.

Example:

Save the details that make future cards feel personal.

Subheadings should not repeat the headline.

They should add useful context.

---

# Body Copy

Body copy should be concise.

Use short paragraphs.

Avoid long explanations unless the user specifically needs guidance.

Body copy should explain:

What this is.

Why it matters.

What happens next.

Example:

Add a few memories now, and future cards will feel more personal with less effort.

---

# Button Labels

Button labels should be action oriented.

Use verbs.

Good examples:

Start Card

Review Draft

Add Person

Log Memory

Save Changes

Open Calendar

View Plan

Update Payment Method

Avoid:

Submit

Proceed

Execute

Confirm Action

Click Here

OK, unless used in simple confirmations.

---

# Primary Button Rules

Primary buttons should describe the main action.

They should never be vague.

Instead of:

Continue

Use:

Continue to Review

Instead of:

Save

Use:

Save Memory

when context is not obvious.

---

# Secondary Button Rules

Secondary buttons should describe alternative actions.

Examples:

Review Memories

Maybe Later

Clear Search

View All Cards

Secondary buttons should never sound equally urgent as the primary button.

---

# Link Copy

Links should be specific.

Good examples:

View all upcoming cards.

Manage billing.

Open notification settings.

Learn how Autopilot works.

Avoid:

Here.

More.

Click here.

Read more, unless no clearer alternative exists.

---

# Empty State Copy

Empty states should feel encouraging.

They should explain what will appear once the user adds information.

Structure:

Headline.

Supporting sentence.

Primary action.

Optional secondary action.

Example:

No memories yet.

Save small moments now, and future cards will feel much more personal.

Log a Memory

---

# Error Message Copy

Error messages should be reassuring and practical.

Structure:

What happened.

Whether anything was lost.

What to do next.

Example:

We could not save that memory.

Nothing was lost. Please try again.

Avoid:

Error 500.

Request failed.

Invalid operation.

Something went wrong, unless no detail is available.

---

# Success Message Copy

Success messages should confirm the completed action clearly.

Examples:

Memory saved.

Card scheduled.

Recipient updated.

Settings saved.

Payment method updated.

Avoid excessive celebration.

The product should feel premium and calm.

---

# Loading Message Copy

Loading messages should be subtle.

Examples:

Preparing your recommendations...

Loading memories...

Finding your cards...

Getting things ready...

Avoid:

Please wait.

Processing request.

Fetching data.

Running AI.

---

# Search Copy

Search should feel like discovery.

Preferred placeholders:

Search people, memories, cards...

Search this relationship...

Search settings...

Search cards...

Search memories...

No results copy:

Nothing matched your search.

Try another name, memory, occasion, or keyword.

Avoid:

No records found.

Zero matching entities.

Query returned no results.

---

# AI Concierge Copy

AI Concierge copy should be helpful, humble, and specific.

Examples:

This memory may help make the card more personal.

You recently saved a detail that could work well here.

I could not find any recent memories for this relationship.

Would you like to add one?

Avoid:

The AI believes.

The model recommends.

Based on algorithmic confidence.

Semantic analysis found.

---

# Notification Copy

Notifications should be clear and useful.

Good examples:

Sarah's birthday card is ready to review.

Your card to Dad has been delivered.

Emily's anniversary is coming up next week.

A draft needs your approval before it can be mailed.

Avoid vague notifications:

Reminder.

Update available.

Action needed.

Something changed.

---

# Card Creation Copy

Card creation copy should reassure users that they remain in control.

Preferred language:

Review and edit before anything is mailed.

Use this memory.

Make it warmer.

Try another version.

Save Draft.

Approve Card.

Avoid:

Generate final output.

Submit message.

AI completed card.

Send automatically without clear approval.

---

# Onboarding Copy

Onboarding should explain why information matters.

Example:

Favorite memories help us write cards that sound personal instead of generic.

Reassurance:

You can always add more later.

Avoid making onboarding feel like a long form.

---

# Authentication Copy

Authentication copy should be secure, calm, and simple.

Examples:

Welcome back.

Create your account.

Check your email.

Reset your password.

We sent you a secure sign in link.

Avoid playful or overly casual security language.

---

# Billing Copy

Billing copy must be transparent.

Examples:

Your plan renews on July 18.

You have 6 cards remaining.

Update payment method.

View invoices.

Manage subscription.

Avoid pressure based upgrade language.

Do not use fear based billing copy.

---

# Settings Copy

Settings copy should describe outcomes.

Instead of:

Configure notification preferences.

Use:

Choose when you want to hear from us.

Instead of:

Manage Autopilot parameters.

Use:

Decide how Autopilot helps with upcoming cards.

---

# Calendar Copy

Calendar copy should focus on preparation.

Examples:

Coming up soon.

Needs a card.

Draft ready.

Scheduled.

Completed.

Missed occasions should be handled gently.

Example:

This occasion has passed. A belated card can still feel thoughtful.

---

# Recipient Profile Copy

Recipient profile copy should feel personal.

Use:

Memories.

Timeline.

Important dates.

Things to remember.

Things to avoid.

Previous cards.

Avoid:

Recipient data.

Profile fields.

Relationship object.

Metadata.

---

# Dashboard Copy

Dashboard copy should help users decide what matters today.

Examples:

Here is what deserves your attention.

Your upcoming cards are on track.

Everything looks up to date.

A few thoughtful opportunities are coming up.

Avoid turning the dashboard into task management language.

---

# Confirmation Dialog Copy

Confirmation dialogs should be direct.

Structure:

Clear headline.

Short explanation.

Primary action.

Secondary action.

Example:

Delete this draft?

This cannot be undone.

Delete Draft

Cancel

---

# Warning Dialog Copy

Warnings should be calm but clear.

Example:

This card is scheduled to be mailed tomorrow.

If you make changes now, we may need to update the mailing schedule.

Review Schedule

Keep Editing

---

# Validation Message Copy

Validation should help users correct mistakes.

Examples:

Enter a valid email address.

Password must be at least 8 characters.

Choose a date before continuing.

Add a name for this person.

Avoid:

Invalid input.

Field required.

Wrong format.

---

# Accessibility Language

Accessible labels should be descriptive.

Examples:

Close search.

Open notification settings.

Remove birthday filter.

Review card for Sarah.

Avoid labels like:

Button.

Icon.

Close.

More, unless context is programmatically clear.

---

# Inclusive Language

Copy should be inclusive across:

Families.

Friendships.

Romantic relationships.

Professional relationships.

Chosen family.

Caregivers.

Complex relationships.

Avoid assuming:

Every user is married.

Every parent relationship is simple.

Every holiday is celebrated.

Every family structure is traditional.

Use flexible language whenever possible.

---

# Reading Level

Product copy should be easy to understand.

Target reading level:

Plain, everyday English.

Avoid legalistic or technical phrasing unless required.

---

# Capitalization Rules

Use sentence case for:

Headlines.

Buttons.

Labels.

Navigation.

Empty states.

Notifications.

Examples:

Create card.

Review draft.

Add person.

Search memories.

Do not use title case unless required by brand names, legal names, or proper nouns.

---

# Date and Time Formatting

Use clear, human friendly dates.

Examples:

Today.

Tomorrow.

July 18.

July 18, 2026.

In 3 days.

Avoid ambiguous formats:

07/18/26.

18/07/26.

Use relative dates only when they improve clarity.

---

# Number Formatting

Use simple number formatting.

Examples:

6 cards remaining.

21 upcoming occasions.

3 memories saved.

Use numerals for counts.

Avoid spelling out numbers in UI labels when speed matters.

---

# Personalization Guidelines

Personalized copy should use names carefully.

Good:

Sarah's birthday is coming up.

Dad's card is ready to review.

Avoid over personalization that feels intrusive.

Do not repeat names excessively in the same view.

---

# Localization Readiness

Copy should be written so it can eventually be translated.

Avoid:

Idioms.

Puns.

Culture specific jokes.

Text embedded inside images.

Hard coded sentence fragments that cannot be reordered.

All user facing strings should be centralized where practical.

---

# Acceptance Criteria

The Copy and Content System is complete when:

All user facing language follows the Relationship Concierge voice.

Headlines, buttons, links, errors, empty states, loading states, notifications, and AI guidance feel consistent.

The product avoids technical, transactional, guilt based, or generic SaaS language.

AI related copy is humble, accurate, and trust preserving.

Sensitive moments are handled gently.

Billing and security copy remain clear and factual.

All copy supports accessibility.

All copy is ready for future localization.

---

# Definition of Done

This specification is complete when:

Every major product surface has clear copy rules.

Every reusable copy pattern has examples.

Implementation teams can write or replace frontend copy without making brand voice decisions.

The redesigned application sounds consistent from onboarding through card delivery.

Every word reinforces F.I. Forgot as a premium Relationship Concierge.
