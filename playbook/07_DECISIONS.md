# Product Decision Framework

This document is the institutional memory of F.I. Forgot.

It records the important decisions that shape the product and explains why they were made.

The goal is not to remember every feature.

The goal is to preserve the thinking behind the product.

A future engineer, designer, product manager, or AI assistant should be able to understand not only what we decided, but why.

Every decision should make the next decision easier.

---

# The Purpose of This Document

Products evolve.

Technology changes.

Customer expectations change.

The reasoning behind good decisions should not disappear.

When someone asks:

"Why is the product built this way?"

The answer should be here.

Not in Slack.

Not in old chat logs.

Not in someone's memory.

---

# Foundational Decisions

Foundational Decisions define the identity of F.I. Forgot.

These decisions should rarely change.

Changing one of these means reconsidering the Constitution, Brand, Customer, Product, or Design philosophy.

They are not impossible to change.

They simply require extraordinary evidence.

---

## FD001

### The Service Is the Product

**Decision**

F.I. Forgot is a subscription relationship service.

The app is the control panel.

The ongoing service is the product.

**Decision Drivers**

Customer.

Brand.

Product.

**Reasoning**

Customers buy confidence, not software.

**Evidence**

Founder vision.

Customer observations.

**Status**

Active.

---

## FD002

### Relationships Before Technology

**Decision**

Relationships always take priority over technology.

Technology exists only to strengthen relationships.

**Reasoning**

Technology changes.

Relationships do not.

**Status**

Active.

---

## FD003

### Trust Before Engagement

**Decision**

We optimize for confidence and trust rather than time spent inside the app.

**Reasoning**

Customers hired us to remove work.

Not create another habit.

**Status**

Active.

---

## FD004

### Automation Before Manual Work

**Decision**

Whenever automation can safely replace customer effort, automation should be preferred.

**Reasoning**

The service should become quieter over time.

**Status**

Active.

---

## FD005

### Every Pixel Earns Its Place

**Decision**

Every visual element must justify why it exists.

Removing clutter is usually better than adding features.

**Reasoning**

Complexity increases mental load.

**Status**

Active.

# Decisions We Hope Never to Reverse

These principles define the company.

Reversing them would likely mean we are building a different product.

The service is the product.

Relationships before technology.

Trust before engagement.

Automation before manual work.

Every pixel earns its place.

Customers should spend less time inside the app every year.

The product should become quieter over time.

# Working Decisions

Working Decisions describe current implementations.

Unlike Foundational Decisions, these are expected to evolve.

Every Working Decision should include enough context so future teams understand why it exists.

---

# Decision Template

Every Working Decision should contain:

**Decision**

What was decided.

**Reasoning**

Why we made this choice.

**Decision Drivers**

Which playbook principles influenced the decision.

**Evidence**

Customer feedback.

Testing.

Analytics.

Engineering constraints.

Founder judgment.

Or another source.

**Alternatives Considered**

What else we discussed.

**Decision Type**

Type 1, difficult to reverse.

Type 2, easy to reverse.

**Status**

Active.

Replaced.

Archived.

Rejected.

Under Evaluation.

**Review Date**

If appropriate.

Otherwise write:

No planned review.

# Rejected Ideas

Rejected ideas are valuable.

Do not delete them.

Document them.

Future teams should understand why something was rejected before suggesting it again.

---

## Example

### RJ001

Daily Streaks

**Decision**

Rejected.

**Reasoning**

Daily streaks encourage engagement instead of confidence.

They reward opening the app rather than strengthening relationships.

**Decision Drivers**

Trust before engagement.

Reduce mental load.

**Status**

Rejected.

**Can This Be Revisited?**

No.

It conflicts with the company's philosophy.

# Open Questions

Not every important discussion requires an immediate answer.

Use this section for ideas that deserve additional research before becoming decisions.

Each question should include:

The question.

Why it matters.

Evidence needed.

Decision owner.

Current status.

Do not confuse uncertainty with indecision.

Good questions deserve documentation.

# Product Authority

The founder defines product vision.

Cursor helps execute it.

Cursor may:

Recommend improvements.

Simplify workflows.

Identify inconsistencies.

Suggest alternatives.

Challenge assumptions with reasoning.

Cursor may not redefine the company's philosophy without explicit approval.

The playbook is the source of truth.

If a recommendation conflicts with the playbook, the playbook wins unless it is intentionally updated first.

---

# When to Create a Decision

A new decision should be documented whenever:

A foundational product philosophy changes.

A major workflow changes.

A significant feature is accepted or rejected.

A pricing model changes.

A navigation model changes.

A customer experience changes.

A recurring debate has finally been resolved.

If the answer to "Why did we do this?" would be important six months from now, record it here.

---

# Decision Review

Before changing an existing decision, ask:

Has customer behavior changed?

Has technology changed?

Has our understanding improved?

Does this still align with the Constitution?

Does this still align with our Brand?

Does this still reduce effort?

Does this still increase confidence?

Never change a decision simply because something newer exists.

Change it because something better exists.

---

# The North Star

A good decision makes future decisions easier.

A great decision strengthens the product without adding complexity.

The best decisions become principles that no longer need to be debated.

This document exists to preserve those principles, so the product can evolve without losing its identity.
