# Content Strategy

---

# Purpose

Words are part of the product.

Every sentence in F.I. Forgot should build trust, reduce effort, and encourage thoughtfulness.

The interface does not simply communicate information.

It creates an emotional experience.

The purpose of this document is to establish a single content strategy that governs every word across the product.

Every piece of content should answer one question:

**"What would a world class Relationship Concierge say?"**

---

# Content Philosophy

Every word should have a purpose.

Content should never exist simply to fill space.

Good content explains.

Great content reassures.

Exceptional content strengthens relationships.

The product should sound like:

A thoughtful concierge.

Not software.

Not marketing.

Not artificial intelligence.

---

# Our Voice

The F.I. Forgot voice is:

Warm.

Professional.

Confident.

Calm.

Helpful.

Optimistic.

Emotionally intelligent.

Respectful.

Thoughtful.

Never dramatic.

Never loud.

Never sales driven.

---

# Tone Principles

The tone should adapt to the situation.

### Welcoming

During onboarding.

"We're glad you're here."

---

### Encouraging

When users build relationships.

"Every story you save makes future cards more personal."

---

### Reassuring

When something goes wrong.

"Your draft is safe."

---

### Celebratory

When something meaningful happens.

"Your card is on its way."

---

### Calm

When users are waiting.

"We're preparing your draft."

---

### Respectful

When discussing personal relationships.

Always assume emotional significance.

---

# Product Vocabulary

Certain words should be used consistently.

Use:

People

Relationships

Memories

Moments

Stories

Thoughtful

Concierge

Timeline

Upcoming Moments

Autopilot

Brownie Points

Avoid replacing established terminology with synonyms.

Consistency improves confidence.

---

# Words To Avoid

Avoid language that feels technical.

Instead of:

Database

Record

Object

Metadata

AI Output

Prompt

Operation

Entity

Algorithm

Machine Learning

Use:

Person

Relationship

Memory

Story

Draft

Suggestion

Recommendation

Details

Information

---

# Speaking About AI

AI should remain invisible whenever possible.

Do not say:

"Our AI generated this."

Prefer:

"We created a thoughtful first draft."

Or:

"We used what you've shared to create a starting point."

Relationships remain the hero.

---

# Headlines

Headlines should be:

Short.

Clear.

Human.

Emotionally positive.

Examples:

Your People

Upcoming Moments

Your Timeline

Thoughtful Ideas

Your Draft Is Ready

Avoid:

Dashboard Overview

Analytics

Relationship Metrics

Generated Results

---

# Supporting Copy

Supporting copy should explain:

Why this matters.

What happens next.

How the user benefits.

Keep paragraphs short.

Avoid unnecessary explanation.

---

# Calls To Action

Buttons should begin with verbs.

Examples:

Add Person

Log Memory

Create Card

Review Draft

Enable Autopilot

View Timeline

Import Contacts

Avoid:

Continue

Submit

Execute

Process

Complete

Unless the action truly matches the wording.

---

# Empty States

Empty state copy should:

Reduce anxiety.

Encourage progress.

Explain value.

Never imply failure.

Example:

"Every relationship starts with a single story."

Not:

"No memories found."

---

# Error Messages

Error messages should:

Explain clearly.

Take responsibility.

Offer recovery.

Avoid blame.

Good:

"We couldn't finish that just yet."

Poor:

"Request failed."

---

# Success Messages

Success messages should:

Confirm completion.

Provide reassurance.

Encourage the next step.

Example:

"Memory saved."

"This will help future cards feel even more personal."

---

# Notifications

Notifications should feel helpful.

Not demanding.

Good:

"Emily's birthday is next week."

Better:

"Emily's birthday is next week. There's still plenty of time to create something thoughtful."

---

# Emails

Emails should sound like personal assistance.

Never marketing blasts.

Each email should answer:

Why am I receiving this?

Why does it matter?

What should I do next?

---

# Business Communication

Business mode should remain warm while becoming more professional.

Example:

"You have three important client anniversaries this month."

Not:

"CRM Reminder."

Professional relationships deserve thoughtful language too.

---

# Reading Level

Target reading level:

Approximately eighth grade.

Sentences should be:

Short.

Direct.

Easy to scan.

Avoid unnecessarily complex vocabulary.

---

# Sentence Length

Mix short and medium length sentences.

Avoid long paragraphs.

Break information naturally.

Whitespace improves readability.

---

# Lists

Use lists only when they improve clarity.

Limit list length whenever possible.

Keep wording parallel.

---

# Numbers

Use numbers when they improve understanding.

Examples:

3 upcoming birthdays

2 cards in progress

Avoid unnecessary precision.

Prefer:

About a week ago.

Instead of:

7 days ago.

When emotional context matters more than exact dates.

---

# Dates

Display dates consistently.

Examples:

April 15

October 3, 2026

Yesterday

Today

Tomorrow

Prefer relative dates when they improve readability.

---

# Time

Use conversational time references.

Examples:

In two days.

Next week.

This afternoon.

Avoid military or technical formatting unless appropriate.

---

# Personalization

Content should adapt naturally.

Example:

Welcome back, James.

Emily's birthday is coming up.

Because you've added several memories, your next card will feel even more personal.

Never personalize in surprising ways.

---

# Humor

Humor should be rare.

Use only when:

Appropriate.

Gentle.

Universally understandable.

Never joke about:

Relationships.

Loss.

Birthdays.

Mistakes.

Payments.

Privacy.

Errors.

---

# Inclusivity

Content should welcome everyone.

Avoid assumptions about:

Families.

Relationships.

Gender.

Culture.

Religion.

Background.

Celebrate diversity without calling unnecessary attention to differences.

---

# Accessibility

Content should support accessibility.

Requirements:

Simple language.

Clear headings.

Meaningful labels.

Descriptive buttons.

Logical reading order.

No emoji only communication.

No instructions based only on color.

---

# Localization

Write copy that is easy to translate.

Avoid:

Idioms.

Regional slang.

Wordplay.

Cultural references.

Unnecessary abbreviations.

Future localization should require minimal rewriting.

---

# Consistency Rules

The same action should always use the same wording.

Example:

Never alternate between:

Recipient

Person

Contact

Relationship

If the feature refers to the same concept.

Consistency builds confidence.

---

# Microcopy Principles

Small pieces of text matter.

Examples include:

Helper text.

Tooltips.

Validation.

Loading messages.

Button labels.

Every word should reduce uncertainty.

---

# Tooltips

Tooltips should explain.

Not teach.

Maximum:

Two short sentences.

Avoid lengthy documentation inside tooltips.

---

# Placeholders

Placeholders should provide examples.

Never instructions.

Good:

"The road trip to Maine."

Poor:

"Enter memory."

---

# Progressive Disclosure

Explain only what users need right now.

Do not overwhelm users with long explanations.

Allow curiosity to reveal deeper information.

---

# Writing Checklist

Every piece of content should be:

Clear.

Concise.

Helpful.

Human.

Warm.

Respectful.

Accessible.

Consistent.

Action oriented.

Relationship focused.

---

# Anti Patterns

Never use:

Technical jargon.

Corporate buzzwords.

Marketing hype.

Artificial enthusiasm.

Passive aggressive language.

Blame.

Fear.

Guilt.

Unnecessary exclamation marks.

Long walls of text.

Never sound like:

Enterprise software.

Documentation.

Customer support tickets.

Artificial intelligence.

---

# Final Editorial Test

Before publishing any content, ask:

Would a thoughtful concierge naturally say this?

Would this reduce uncertainty?

Would this strengthen trust?

Does this sound warm?

Could this sentence be shorter?

Does this respect the user's time?

Would this still feel appropriate five years from now?

Would I say this to someone I genuinely cared about?

If every answer is yes, the content meets the F.I. Forgot standard.

---

# Closing Principle

Every word in F.I. Forgot should make the product feel a little more human.

Technology should fade into the background.

Relationships should remain at the center.

That is the purpose of our content strategy.
