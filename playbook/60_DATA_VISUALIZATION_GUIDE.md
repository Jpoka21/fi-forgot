# Data Visualization Guide

---

# Purpose

Data should tell a story.

In F.I. Forgot, numbers are never the product.

Relationships are.

Every visualization should help users better understand the people who matter most without overwhelming them with statistics.

Charts, timelines, progress indicators, and metrics should always answer one question:

**"How can we help someone become more thoughtful?"**

If a visualization does not improve understanding or encourage thoughtful action, it should not exist.

---

# Visualization Philosophy

Most software visualizes activity.

F.I. Forgot visualizes relationships.

The goal is never to impress users with analytics.

The goal is to quietly surface insights that help them become more intentional.

Every visualization should be:

* emotionally meaningful

* immediately understandable

* visually calm

* action oriented

* human centered

* beautiful without being decorative

---

# Relationship Concierge Approach

A Relationship Concierge would never overwhelm someone with charts.

Instead it would gently highlight:

* who may need attention

* what has improved

* what deserves celebration

* where thoughtful opportunities exist

The visualization should always support action.

Never curiosity alone.

---

# Emotional Goals

Visualizations should create:

## Clarity

Users understand information instantly.

---

## Confidence

Users trust the information.

---

## Encouragement

Progress feels achievable.

---

## Awareness

Important relationships naturally stand out.

---

## Calm

The interface never feels like a business intelligence dashboard.

---

# Visualization Principles

Every visualization should be:

Simple.

Readable.

Actionable.

Minimal.

Consistent.

Accessible.

Meaningful.

Avoid decorative charts that communicate little value.

---

# Hierarchy

Every visualization should answer three questions.

## What happened?

Example:

You sent three cards this month.

---

## Why does it matter?

Example:

Those cards strengthened three important relationships.

---

## What should I do next?

Example:

Emily's birthday is next week.

---

# Preferred Visualization Types

Use only visualization types that improve understanding.

Preferred:

* Progress Rings

* Progress Bars

* Timelines

* Activity Feeds

* Relationship Maps

* Status Chips

* Trend Lines

* Heat Maps

* Lists

* Milestone Cards

* Calendar Views

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

---

# Charts To Avoid

Do not use:

3D charts

Radar charts

Exploding pie charts

Gauge charts

Speedometers

Complex financial dashboards

Decorative infographics

Charts requiring legends to understand

---

# Relationship Health Visualization

Relationship Health is one of the product's most important visualizations.

It should communicate:

* growth

* opportunity

* momentum

Never judgment.

Preferred visualization:

Circular progress ring.

Supporting copy:

"This relationship is becoming more personal."

Avoid:

Letter grades.

Percentages without explanation.

Red warning states.

---

# Brownie Points Visualization

Brownie Points should feel playful but premium.

Preferred display:

Animated count

Progress bar toward next milestone

Subtle milestone indicators

Never:

Casino style animations

Leaderboards

Aggressive gamification

---

# Relationship Timeline

Timeline is a storytelling visualization.

It should display:

Cards sent

Memories added

Questions answered

Life updates

Important events

Timeline should feel chronological and personal.

Not transactional.

---

# Upcoming Events Visualization

Preferred format:

Vertical timeline.

Display:

Today

Tomorrow

This Week

Later This Month

Each event should clearly show:

Person

Occasion

Preparation status

Action available

---

# Card Activity

Display:

Draft

Ordered

Handwritten

Sent

Delivered

Use a horizontal progress indicator.

Users should immediately understand where a card is in the journey.

---

# Memory Visualization

Memories should appear as rich timeline cards.

Each card includes:

Date

Story

Photos when available

Tags

Related card history

Relationship context

Never reduce memories to spreadsheets.

---

# Dashboard Metrics

Dashboard metrics should remain minimal.

Recommended cards:

People

Upcoming Moments

Cards In Progress

Autopilot Status

Relationship Opportunities

Avoid displaying excessive KPIs.

Relationships are more important than metrics.

---

# Progress Indicators

Use progress indicators only when users benefit from understanding completion.

Examples:

Onboarding

Profile completion

Card creation

Import process

AI generation

Progress should feel encouraging.

Never stressful.

---

# Circular Progress

Use for:

Relationship Health

Onboarding completion

Autopilot setup

Account completeness

Maximum one or two circles per screen.

---

# Linear Progress

Use for:

Uploading

Importing

Saving

Questionnaires

Generation

Linear progress should clearly indicate remaining work.

---

# Step Indicators

Use for:

Multi step onboarding

Card ordering

Business setup

Display:

Completed

Current

Upcoming

Keep labels visible.

---

# Trend Indicators

Show trends only when meaningful.

Examples:

More memories this month

Relationship Health improving

More thoughtful consistency

Use subtle arrows.

Avoid dramatic green and red stock market styling.

---

# Calendar Visualization

Calendar should emphasize people.

Every event card includes:

Person

Occasion

Relationship

Preparation status

Quick action

Avoid cluttered calendars.

---

# Heat Maps

Future capability.

Use heat maps only when they answer useful questions.

Example:

Months with the most thoughtful activity.

Never display heat maps simply because they look impressive.

---

# Lists As Visualizations

Sometimes lists outperform charts.

Examples:

People needing attention

Recent memories

Upcoming birthdays

Relationship opportunities

Lists should remain the default visualization unless a chart clearly improves understanding.

---

# Status Indicators

Status should be represented consistently.

Examples:

Draft

Scheduled

Ordered

Sent

Delivered

Archived

Use chips rather than long text whenever practical.

---

# Icons

Icons support understanding.

Never replace labels.

Icons should remain consistent with the Iconography Guide.

---

# Color Philosophy

Color communicates meaning.

Use:

Warm neutrals

Relationship accent colors

Gentle success tones

Subtle warning tones

Avoid bright red and bright green dashboards.

Relationship Health should never rely only on color.

---

# Data Density

Every screen should breathe.

Avoid information overload.

Prefer:

One meaningful insight

Over

Ten minor statistics.

Whitespace improves comprehension.

---

# Personalization

Visualizations should evolve.

New users see:

Simple summaries.

Experienced users see:

More historical context.

Business users see:

Additional professional insights.

The interface grows with the user.

---

# Empty Visualization States

Every visualization should gracefully support:

No data

Not enough data

Loading

Offline

Error

Each state should follow the Empty States and Error States specifications.

---

# Loading States

Charts should use skeleton placeholders.

Avoid flashing empty containers.

Maintain layout stability during loading.

---

# Responsive Behavior

Desktop:

Multiple visualizations may appear side by side.

Tablet:

Balanced two column layouts.

Mobile:

Single column.

Charts should never require horizontal scrolling.

---

# Motion Guidelines

Animation should reinforce understanding.

Good examples:

Progress ring fills gently.

Timeline fades into view.

Bars animate once.

Numbers count smoothly.

Avoid:

Looping animation

Constant movement

Exaggerated effects

Charts should become still after loading.

---

# Accessibility Requirements

Every visualization must satisfy WCAG AA.

Requirements:

* sufficient contrast

* descriptive labels

* screen reader summaries

* keyboard accessibility

* patterns in addition to color

* touch friendly targets

* reduced motion support

* logical reading order

* text alternatives for charts

Every chart must remain understandable without color alone.

---

# Performance Expectations

Visualizations should appear responsive.

Goals:

Initial render

Less than 300 milliseconds

Animation

200 to 400 milliseconds

Dashboard interaction

Instant

Avoid expensive visualizations that reduce performance.

---

# Copy Guidelines

Titles should describe relationships.

Examples:

Upcoming Moments

Relationship Growth

Cards In Progress

Recent Memories

People Who May Appreciate A Card

Avoid:

Analytics

Reports

Metrics

Statistics

Performance

Insights should be written in complete human sentences whenever possible.

---

# Anti Patterns

Never overwhelm users with numbers.

Never create dashboards that resemble financial software.

Never prioritize charts over relationships.

Never use visualization simply because data exists.

Never require users to interpret complex legends.

Never display meaningless percentages.

Never use red to shame users.

Never compare relationships competitively.

Never rank loved ones.

Never encourage treating relationships like a game.

---

# Review Checklist

Every visualization should answer:

□ Does it help users become more thoughtful?

□ Is it immediately understandable?

□ Does it encourage action?

□ Is the visualization emotionally appropriate?

□ Does it avoid unnecessary complexity?

□ Does it prioritize relationships over numbers?

□ Is it accessible?

□ Does it work beautifully on mobile?

□ Is animation subtle?

□ Does it remain useful as more data is collected?

□ Does it support empty, loading, offline, and error states?

□ Does it feel worthy of a premium Relationship Concierge?

If every answer is yes, the visualization meets the F.I. Forgot standard.
