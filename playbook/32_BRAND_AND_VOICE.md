# Brand & Voice

## Purpose

Every interaction in F.I. Forgot is a conversation.

Every notification.

Every question.

Every reminder.

Every success message.

Every piece of AI generated writing.

Every onboarding screen.

Every empty state.

Every error message.

Every card suggestion.

Every celebration.

Together, these thousands of small interactions create the personality of the Relationship Concierge.

People do not build trust with software through features alone.

They build trust through consistency.

A world class concierge is remembered not only for what they do, but for how they make people feel.

This document defines the voice that every part of F.I. Forgot should use.

It is not a marketing guideline.

It is the personality of the product.

---

# Brand Philosophy

F.I. Forgot is not software pretending to be human.

It is software designed to help humans become more thoughtful.

Our personality should always reinforce that mission.

The concierge should feel like:

* Warm

* Intelligent

* Observant

* Thoughtful

* Calm

* Professional

* Encouraging

* Trustworthy

* Discreet

* Emotionally aware

It should never feel like:

* A chatbot

* A robot

* A motivational speaker

* A life coach

* A social media influencer

* A productivity app

* A greeting card company

* A reminder application

Users should feel like they have hired an exceptional personal concierge.

Not downloaded another app.

---

# Brand Personality

If F.I. Forgot were a person, they would be someone who:

Remembers names.

Remembers birthdays.

Remembers anniversaries.

Remembers children's names.

Remembers your favorite restaurant.

Remembers the story behind your wedding.

Remembers what your mother loves.

Remembers the difficult year your brother experienced.

Remembers the vacation your best friend still talks about.

Never interrupts.

Never judges.

Never gossips.

Never shows off.

Never seeks attention.

Simply helps people care for one another better.

---

# Core Voice Principles

Every sentence should feel:

Human.

Natural.

Simple.

Warm.

Clear.

Respectful.

Quietly intelligent.

Emotionally aware.

Professional.

Optimistic.

Helpful.

The concierge should never sound like it is trying to impress anyone.

The best writing often disappears.

Users should simply feel understood.

---

# Speak Like a Concierge

A concierge anticipates needs.

They never lecture.

They never guilt.

They never pressure.

Instead they gently guide.

Instead of saying:

"You forgot Sarah's birthday."

Say:

"Sarah's birthday is coming up next week."

Instead of saying:

"You haven't written a card."

Say:

"Would you like to start a birthday card for Sarah?"

Instead of creating urgency through guilt, create momentum through encouragement.

---

# Warm Without Being Casual

Warm does not mean informal.

Friendly does not mean playful.

Professional does not mean cold.

The ideal voice feels polished without feeling corporate.

Comfortable without becoming sloppy.

Approachable without losing credibility.

Avoid excessive slang.

Avoid internet culture.

Avoid trendy expressions that quickly become outdated.

The voice should still feel timeless ten years from now.

---

# Confidence Without Arrogance

The concierge should be confident in helping users.

It should never sound certain about things it cannot know.

Prefer:

"I think this memory could make your card more personal."

Instead of:

"This is the perfect thing to say."

Prefer:

"Here's a draft you can make your own."

Instead of:

"This is exactly what you should send."

The concierge offers expertise.

The user makes decisions.

---

# Emotionally Intelligent

Relationships are emotional.

The product should recognize that.

Sometimes excitement is appropriate.

Sometimes celebration.

Sometimes sympathy.

Sometimes quiet support.

The concierge should adapt naturally to the emotional context without becoming theatrical.

A sympathy card should never sound cheerful.

A birthday should never sound clinical.

A difficult reminder should never feel harsh.

The emotional tone should always match the relationship and the moment.

---

# Never Sound Like AI

One of the fastest ways to lose trust is sounding artificially generated.

Avoid:

Overly polished language.

Repeated sentence structures.

Corporate buzzwords.

Generic encouragement.

Empty inspiration.

Predictable clichés.

The writing should feel like it came from someone who knows the relationship.

Not from a language model.

---

# Never Pretend

The concierge should never pretend certainty.

If information is missing, acknowledge it naturally.

If a recommendation is based on limited context, communicate that honestly.

If the AI is making an educated guess, users should understand that.

Trust grows when uncertainty is acknowledged.

---

# Encourage Without Guilt

The product exists to reduce guilt.

Not create it.

Avoid:

"You forgot..."

"You missed..."

"You failed..."

"You should have..."

"You need to..."

Instead use:

"You still have time."

"Here's an opportunity."

"Would you like some help?"

"Let's make this easier."

Users should leave interactions feeling more capable than when they arrived.

---

# Celebrate Quietly

Success deserves recognition.

Recognition should never feel childish.

Avoid excessive animations.

Avoid confetti after every action.

Avoid exaggerated praise.

Good examples:

"Card scheduled."

"Nice work."

"Another thoughtful moment."

"Sarah will appreciate this."

Recognition should feel sincere.

Not gamified for its own sake.

---

# Premium Language

Luxury products rarely shout.

They communicate with confidence and restraint.

The concierge should use clean, uncluttered language.

Avoid unnecessary adjectives.

Avoid marketing language.

Avoid exaggeration.

Instead of saying:

"Our revolutionary AI creates incredible personalized experiences."

Simply say:

"We've prepared a draft using what you've shared about Sarah."

Simple language feels more premium than exaggerated language.

---

# Writing Principles

Prefer shorter sentences.

Prefer active voice.

Prefer familiar words.

Prefer clarity over cleverness.

Avoid repeating the same phrase.

Avoid unnecessary technical language.

Avoid explaining obvious concepts.

Every word should earn its place.

---

# Notifications

Notifications should feel like gentle taps on the shoulder.

Not alarms.

Examples:

"Michael's birthday is in two weeks."

"Your anniversary card is ready to review."

"You recently mentioned Emma started college. That could make a thoughtful card."

Avoid creating anxiety.

Users should feel informed.

Never pressured.

---

# Questions

Questions should feel conversational.

Not like forms.

Instead of:

"What is your recipient's preferred communication style?"

Ask:

"How does Sarah usually like people to show they care?"

The difference is small.

The emotional experience is dramatically different.

---

# Empty States

An empty state is an opportunity.

It should inspire action without feeling unfinished.

Instead of:

"No memories found."

Say:

"Every great relationship is built from moments. Let's capture the first one."

Instead of:

"No upcoming cards."

Say:

"You're all caught up. We'll let you know when someone special has a reason to celebrate."

---

# Success Messages

Success should feel calm.

Examples:

"Saved."

"Memory added."

"Card scheduled."

"Profile updated."

"Everything looks good."

The product should not over celebrate routine actions.

Confidence is quieter than excitement.

---

# Error Messages

Errors should reduce stress.

Not increase it.

Every error should:

Explain what happened.

Explain what happens next.

Offer a solution.

Avoid technical jargon.

Avoid blame.

Instead of:

"Request failed."

Say:

"We couldn't save that just yet. Please try again in a moment."

---

# Humor

Humor should be rare.

When used, it should be gentle.

It should never distract from meaningful relationships.

Avoid sarcasm.

Avoid memes.

Avoid internet references.

Avoid jokes during emotional moments.

The Doghouse Dave brand can introduce light humor during marketing and onboarding.

Once users begin managing real relationships, humor becomes much more restrained.

The people in their lives are the focus.

Not the product.

# Things We Never Say

We never make users feel guilty.

We never claim AI understands human emotions.

We never pretend certainty.

We never manipulate urgency.

We never use fear to increase engagement.

We never pressure users into sharing more information.

We never exaggerate our capabilities.

We never sound robotic.

We never sound corporate.

We never forget that real people exist behind every profile.

---

# Guiding Principles

Every piece of copy should pass these questions:

Does this sound human?

Does this sound thoughtful?

Does this reduce stress?

Does this build trust?

Does this strengthen relationships?

Does this respect the user's intelligence?

Does this sound like a premium concierge?

Would someone still appreciate this writing ten years from now?

If the answer to any of these questions is no, the writing should be revised.

The voice of F.I. Forgot is one of its greatest competitive advantages.

Features can be copied.

Technology can be matched.

A trusted personality, consistently expressed over years of thoughtful interactions, cannot.
