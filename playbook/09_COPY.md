# Communication Philosophy

Words are part of the product.

Every sentence should reduce uncertainty.

Increase confidence.

Reduce effort.

Strengthen trust.

Customers should never have to decode what we mean.

Good communication is not decoration.

It is part of the service.

---

# Every Word Earns Its Place

Just as every pixel earns its place, every word earns its place.

Every sentence should accomplish at least one of these goals:

Explain.

Guide.

Reassure.

Reduce uncertainty.

Increase confidence.

If removing a sentence improves understanding, remove it.

If a word sounds impressive but communicates less clearly, replace it.

Clarity always wins.

---

# Our Voice

Our voice should feel like someone dependable has already taken care of something important.

We are:

Warm.

Calm.

Clear.

Confident.

Human.

Thoughtful.

Reliable.

Never:

Corporate.

Cold.

Overly clever.

Robotic.

Technical.

Self important.

Overly enthusiastic.

Customers should feel like they are talking to a trusted service, not a piece of software.

---

# The Voice Pyramid

Every piece of communication should be:

Clear.

Then warm.

Then human.

Then, when appropriate, quietly delightful.

Never reverse that order.

Understanding comes before personality.

Trust comes before humor.

---

# Write Like a Human

Use familiar words.

Write short sentences.

Write short paragraphs.

Use contractions naturally.

Choose everyday language.

Avoid jargon.

Avoid buzzwords.

Avoid corporate language.

Avoid startup clichés.

Write as if helping a busy friend.

Not presenting to investors.

---

# Write for Busy People

Customers scan.

They rarely read every word.

Design every message for scanning.

One headline.

One idea.

One action.

Respect attention.

If something can be said in fewer words without losing meaning, shorten it.

---

# Emotional Hierarchy

Every message should answer these questions in order:

Do I understand this?

Do I know what happens next?

Can I trust this?

Do I feel better than I did before I read it?

If the first question is not answered, nothing else matters.

---

# Speak Like a Service

Customers hired a service.

Not software.

Not AI.

Not a dashboard.

Our language should reflect that.

Instead of describing systems, describe outcomes.

Instead of explaining technology, explain what the customer can expect.

The customer cares about what happens.

Not how it happens.

---

# Show Outcomes, Not Processes

Prefer:

"We've got it."

"We'll take care of it."

"Everything's ready."

"You're all set."

"Your card is on its way."

Avoid:

"Configuration completed."

"Workflow updated."

"Automation enabled."

"Request processed."

"Synchronization successful."

Software describes processes.

Services describe outcomes.

---

# One Meaning, One Word

Choose one word for each important concept.

Use it consistently.

A Person should never become a Contact somewhere else.

A Relationship should never become a Connection.

Customers should never have to translate our language.

Consistency reduces thinking.

Consistency builds trust.

---

# The Customer Is Never Wrong

Assume good intentions.

Never blame the customer.

Avoid:

"You entered an invalid value."

"You forgot..."

"Incorrect."

Prefer:

"We couldn't save that."

"Something didn't work."

"Let's try again."

Help people move forward.

Never make them feel responsible for struggling.

---

# Calm Over Celebration

Our emotional goal is relief.

Not excitement.

Most success messages should create calm.

Instead of:

"Success!"

Prefer:

"Everything's handled."

"You're all set."

"One less thing to worry about."

Customers should leave feeling lighter, not overstimulated.

---

# Rhythm Matters

Communication should breathe.

Alternate information with action.

Avoid walls of text.

Use whitespace.

Keep paragraphs short.

Every screen should feel effortless to read.

Reading should never feel like work.

---

# Humor

Humor has a purpose.

It reduces tension.

It creates humanity.

It never reduces trust.

Dave carries most of the humor before customers subscribe.

Inside the product, humor should be subtle.

Warm.

Occasional.

Never distracting.

Never sarcastic.

Never at the customer's expense.

---

# Empty States

An empty screen should never feel empty.

It should answer three questions:

Why is nothing here?

What happens next?

What should I do?

Every empty state should feel like an invitation.

Not an error.

---

# Success Messages

Success messages should create confidence.

Customers should immediately understand:

What happened.

What happens next.

Whether they need to do anything.

Most of the time, the answer should be:

Nothing.

We've got it from here.

---

# Error Messages

Errors should reduce anxiety.

Explain what happened.

Explain what to do next.

Offer a clear path forward.

Avoid technical language whenever possible.

Customers should leave the interaction feeling more confident than when the error appeared.

---

# Notifications

Every notification interrupts someone's life.

Treat that interruption with respect.

Before sending anything, ask:

Does the customer truly need this?

Does it increase confidence?

Does it reduce uncertainty?

Could silence create a better experience?

Silence is often the better product.

---

# AI Generated Writing

The purpose of AI writing is not beautiful writing.

It is believable writing.

Recipients should never think:

"An AI wrote this."

They should think:

"This sounds exactly like them."

The customer's voice always comes before the model's creativity.

---

# Words We Prefer

Handled.

Ready.

Remembered.

Thoughtful.

Relationship.

Together.

We'll.

You're.

Simple.

Calm.

Confident.

Quietly.

---

# Words We Avoid

Workflow.

Platform.

Optimize.

Leverage.

Utilize.

Disruptive.

Revolutionary.

Best in class.

AI powered.

Cutting edge.

Configuration.

Dashboard.

Engagement.

Synchronization.

If a word sounds like it belongs in enterprise software, question whether it belongs here.

---

# Communication Filters

Before publishing any copy, ask:

Is it clear?

Is it warm?

Is it calm?

Does it sound human?

Would Grandma understand it?

Could someone understand it while distracted?

Can it be shorter?

Does it reduce uncertainty?

Does it increase confidence?

Does it sound like someone dependable has their back?

If not, keep editing.

---

# The Communication North Star

Customers should never finish reading our words thinking:

"That was well written."

They should finish thinking:

"I know what's happening."

"I trust them."

"One less thing to worry about."

Every sentence should leave the customer feeling that someone dependable is quietly taking care of something important.

When our words consistently create that feeling, they have done their job.
