# F.I. Forgot Illustration Guide

---

# Purpose

Illustrations are the emotional language of F.I. Forgot.

While typography communicates information and icons communicate actions, illustrations communicate feelings.

They help users recognize themselves in the product.

They transform software into something personal.

Every illustration should reinforce one simple truth:

**Relationships matter.**

---

# Illustration Philosophy

Illustrations are storytelling.

They should never exist merely to decorate a page.

Every illustration should answer one question:

**What emotion should the user feel right now?**

If the illustration does not strengthen that emotion, it does not belong.

---

# Emotional Goals

Illustrations should make users feel:

* Welcome

* Understood

* Hopeful

* Thoughtful

* Comfortable

* Encouraged

* Connected

Illustrations should never create anxiety or pressure.

---

# Brand Personality

F.I. Forgot is warm.

Not childish.

Not corporate.

Not trendy.

Not futuristic.

Illustrations should feel timeless enough that they remain relevant for years without looking dated.

---

# Relationship First

People are always the heroes.

Illustrations should celebrate relationships rather than technology.

The application should never visually emphasize artificial intelligence, automation, or software.

It should emphasize thoughtful human connection.

---

# Dave, The Official Brand Character

Dave is the official mascot of F.I. Forgot.

Dave represents something nearly everyone has experienced.

Forgetting an important moment.

Missing an anniversary.

Realizing a birthday slipped by.

Feeling guilty because life became busy.

Dave exists to communicate empathy.

He is not the product.

He introduces the problem.

The user becomes the hero.

---

# The Story Of Doghouse Dave

Dave is introduced on the homepage sitting inside a doghouse.

The doghouse is symbolic.

It represents those moments when we accidentally let someone down.

The illustration should immediately communicate:

"I've been there."

Users should smile because the situation feels familiar.

Not because Dave is humorous.

The emotional response should be recognition.

Not comedy.

---

# Dave's Role

Dave serves one purpose.

To introduce the problem.

Once the user understands that F.I. Forgot helps them become more thoughtful, Dave steps aside.

Relationships become the focus.

Dave is a guide.

Not the main character.

---

# Where Dave Appears

Dave appears on:

Homepage hero

Marketing materials

Landing pages

Occasional promotional content

Educational content

Brand storytelling

He should appear sparingly elsewhere.

---

# Where Dave Does Not Appear

Dave should not appear during:

Relationship management

Card writing

Recipient profiles

Dashboard workflows

Timeline management

Business Concierge interactions

Billing

Settings

Once users enter the product, their own relationships become the story.

---

# Official Dave Design

Dave is always depicted as:

Early to mid thirties.

Curly, slightly messy dark brown hair.

Light stubble.

Kind facial features.

Warm smile or thoughtful expression.

Dark hoodie.

Blue jeans.

Red Converse style sneakers.

Average build.

Approachable posture.

Dave should always feel relatable.

Never idealized.

Never exaggerated.

---

# Dave's Personality

Dave is:

Thoughtful.

Good hearted.

Slightly forgetful.

Humble.

Kind.

Quietly optimistic.

Dave is never:

Lazy.

Careless.

Immature.

Arrogant.

Goofy.

The joke is never Dave.

The joke is that life gets busy.

---

# Facial Expressions

Expressions should feel genuine.

Gentle smile.

Quiet relief.

Thoughtful reflection.

Gratitude.

Hope.

Occasional embarrassment.

Never exaggerated cartoon expressions.

Never slapstick.

---

# Body Language

Dave should communicate emotion through posture.

Relaxed.

Reflective.

Comfortable.

Natural.

Body language should feel human.

Avoid exaggerated poses.

---

# Illustration Style

Illustrations should feel:

Painterly.

Soft.

Organic.

Warm.

Hand crafted.

Human.

Avoid hyper realism.

Avoid flat corporate vectors.

Avoid exaggerated cartoon proportions.

---

# Color Palette

Illustrations should use the product color system.

Warm neutrals.

Natural greens.

Muted blues.

Soft reds.

Golden sunlight.

Cream backgrounds.

Avoid neon colors.

Avoid overly saturated palettes.

---

# Lighting

Lighting should feel natural.

Morning light.

Golden hour.

Soft indoor lighting.

Avoid dramatic spotlights.

Avoid harsh shadows.

The atmosphere should always feel inviting.

---

# Background Style

Backgrounds should support the story.

Not distract from it.

Minimal environments.

Clean composition.

Thoughtful use of negative space.

---

# Composition

Every illustration should have one focal point.

Visual clutter should be avoided.

Whitespace is encouraged.

The illustration should feel balanced at every screen size.

---

# Relationship Illustrations

Relationship illustrations should celebrate:

Shared meals.

Walks.

Laughing.

Conversations.

Celebrations.

Everyday moments.

Small memories.

The ordinary moments often matter the most.

---

# Homepage Illustration System

The homepage tells a story.

The sequence is:

Problem.

Recognition.

Hope.

Solution.

Dave introduces the problem.

The concierge introduces the solution.

The user's relationships become the future.

---

# Official Homepage Hero

**Asset 001** is the canonical homepage hero illustration.

It depicts Dave sitting in the doghouse.

This asset is the visual foundation of the brand.

Future homepage redesigns should preserve its emotional meaning, even if the composition evolves.

Asset 001 should never be replaced casually.

---

# Dashboard Illustrations

Dashboard illustrations should be minimal.

Relationships remain the focus.

Illustrations should support empty states and helpful moments.

Never compete with actual user content.

---

# Empty State Illustrations

Empty states should feel hopeful.

Examples include:

An empty photo album.

An open notebook.

A blank timeline.

A growing plant.

A mailbox waiting for the next thoughtful card.

The emotion should be possibility.

Not absence.

---

# Celebration Illustrations

Celebrations should feel personal.

Birthdays.

Anniversaries.

Graduations.

Thank you moments.

New babies.

Friendships.

Retirement.

Celebrations should feel intimate rather than extravagant.

---

# Business Concierge Illustrations

Business illustrations should remain relationship focused.

People meeting.

Handwritten notes.

Coffee conversations.

Team appreciation.

Client relationships.

Avoid office clichés.

Avoid skyscrapers.

Avoid endless charts.

---

# Seasonal Illustrations

Seasonal artwork should feel timeless.

Spring.

Summer.

Autumn.

Winter.

Holidays should emphasize relationships rather than decorations.

---

# Diversity And Inclusion

Illustrations should naturally represent a wide variety of people.

Different ages.

Different cultures.

Different family structures.

Different abilities.

Representation should feel authentic.

Never forced.

---

# Accessibility

Illustrations should never communicate essential information alone.

Important actions must always exist in text.

Illustrations support understanding.

They never replace it.

---

# Illustration Asset Library

Every illustration should have:

Unique asset identifier.

Descriptive name.

Purpose.

Location within the application.

Prompt source.

Approval status.

Version history.

Usage notes.

Illustrations should be treated as long term product assets.

---

# Asset Naming Convention

Every illustration receives a permanent identifier.

Example:

Asset 001

Homepage Hero

Asset 002

Welcome Illustration

Asset 003

Relationship Empty State

Asset 004

Memory Timeline

Asset 005

Business Welcome

Identifiers never change.

Illustrations may evolve while preserving their identity.

---

# AI Prompt Standards

Every illustration prompt should describe:

Purpose.

Emotion.

Composition.

Lighting.

Color palette.

Character placement.

Camera perspective.

Environment.

Art style.

Relationship context.

Avoid prompts that focus only on appearance.

The emotion always comes first.

---

# Illustration Review Checklist

Before approving an illustration:

Does it communicate the intended emotion?

Does it feel warm?

Does it feel premium?

Does it feel timeless?

Does it support relationships?

Would it still make sense five years from now?

Does it match Dave's established appearance?

Does it match the official illustration style?

Would users remember the feeling more than the artwork?

If not, revise it.

---

# Things To Avoid

Avoid robots.

Avoid AI imagery.

Avoid glowing technology.

Avoid futuristic interfaces.

Avoid abstract geometric illustrations.

Avoid overly flat corporate vector art.

Avoid exaggerated cartoon proportions.

Avoid memes.

Avoid visual jokes.

Avoid clip art.

Avoid generic stock illustrations.

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

Avoid illustrations that compete with user content.

Avoid making Dave the center of the product after onboarding.

---

# Future Expansion

As F.I. Forgot grows, new illustrations should expand the visual language without changing its identity.

Every new illustration should feel like it belongs to the same family as Asset 001.

The illustration library should become richer over time while remaining unmistakably recognizable as F.I. Forgot.

---

# Final Principle

Illustrations are not decoration.

They are reminders.

They remind users why they opened the app.

Not to send a greeting card.

Not to complete a task.

But to stay connected with the people who matter most.

That is the illustration system of F.I. Forgot.
